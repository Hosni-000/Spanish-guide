# Gender of Spanish Nouns

## 📌 Grammar Rule

Spanish nouns are grammatically **masculine or feminine**. Gender affects articles and adjectives.

The source gives common ending patterns, but emphasizes that they are **general patterns, not absolute rules**.

> ==Learning a noun together with its article is useful.==

## 🧩 Structure

### Common patterns

- Masculine nouns often end in `-o`.
- Feminine nouns often end in `-a`.
- Other endings such as `-e` or consonants can be masculine or feminine.
- Gender determines article choice and affects adjective agreement.

## 📝 Examples

### Basic gender patterns

- `el libro` — the book
- `el perro` — the dog
- `la mesa` — the table
- `la casa` — the house
- `el día` — the day
- `la mano` — the hand
- `el coche` — the car
- `la noche` — the night

### Masculine endings

The source lists these common patterns:

- `-o`: `el libro`, `el perro`
- `-ma`: many Greek-origin nouns: `el problema`, `el tema`
- `-or`: `el amor`, `el calor`
- `-aje`: `el garaje`, `el paisaje`
- `-án` and `-ambre`: `el refrán`, `el hambre`

Other masculine examples:

- `el día`
- `el mapa`
- `el clima`
- `el planeta`
- `el sistema`

Special point from the source:

- `el águila` is **feminine** despite taking `el` in the singular before its stressed initial `a`.

### Feminine endings

- `-a`: `la casa`, `la manzana`
- `-ción` / `-sión`: `la nación`, `la televisión`
- `-dad`: `la ciudad`, `la universidad`

Exceptions include:

- `el día`
- `el mapa`
- Greek-origin masculine nouns such as `el tema` and `el problema`
- `la mano`
- `la radio`

### Nouns with meaning-dependent gender

Some nouns can change gender when their meaning changes.

| Masculine | Meaning | Feminine | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `el capital` | money / financial assets | `la capital` | capital city |
| `el guía` | male guide | `la guía` | female guide / guidebook |
| `el orden` | order, arrangement, tidiness | `la orden` | command / religious order |

### Invariable nouns for people

Some nouns have the same form for male and female people; the article and adjectives show the gender:

- `el cantante / la cantante` — the male/female singer
- `el artista / la artista` — the male/female artist
- `el estudiante / la estudiante` — the male/female student

Examples:

- `El cantante es muy talentoso.` — The male singer is very talented.
- `La cantante es muy talentosa.` — The female singer is very talented.

### Compound noun gender

The source says the gender of compounds is often determined by the main word or by established usage, so it is best to learn each compound with its article.

Examples:

- `el abrelatas` — the can opener
- `el sacapuntas` — the pencil sharpener
- `el paraguas` — the umbrella
- `el lavaplatos` — the dishwasher
- `el cumpleaños` — the birthday
- `el rompecabezas` — the puzzle

`el salvavidas / la salvavidas` can refer to a male/female lifesaver; the object is usually masculine.

### Gender affects articles and adjectives

- Masculine singular: `El libro rojo.` — The red book.
- Feminine singular: `La casa blanca.` — The white house.
- Masculine plural: `Los libros rojos.` — The red books.
- Feminine plural: `Las casas blancas.` — The white houses.
- `Un coche rápido.` — A fast car.
- `Una flor bonita.` — A pretty flower.
- `Unos coches rápidos.` — Some fast cars.
- `Unas flores bonitas.` — Some pretty flowers.

## ⚠️ Important Notes

- Ending patterns are helpful but **not universal**.
- Some nouns simply have to be learned individually.
- Learn nouns with their articles when a gender pattern is unreliable.
- Article and adjective agreement reflects the noun's gender and number.

## 🔄 Conjugation / Forms

### Article agreement

| Gender / number | Definite article | Indefinite article |
|---|---|---|
| masculine singular | `el` | `un` |
| feminine singular | `la` | `una` |
| masculine plural | `los` | `unos` |
| feminine plural | `las` | `unas` |

## 💡 Remember

> ==Gender in Spanish is grammatical, and common endings are patterns rather than absolute rules. Learn uncertain nouns together with `el` or `la`.==
