# Tener que and Deber

## 📌 Grammar Rule

Spanish uses **tener que** and **deber** to express obligation or necessity.

- **tener que** commonly presents an action as necessary or required, often because of circumstances, practical needs, or an external requirement.
- **deber + infinitive** commonly expresses duty, responsibility, or what is advisable.
- **debería + infinitive** is commonly used for advice or recommendation and is softer than a direct obligation.
- **deber de + infinitive** expresses supposition, likelihood, or probability.

> ==The source stresses that the difference between `tener que` and `deber` is not an absolute difference in strength. Context determines how forceful the expression sounds.==

## 🧩 Structure

### Tener que

`tener (conjugated) + que + infinitive`

Example: `Tengo que estudiar.`

### Deber for obligation or advice

`deber + infinitive`

Example: `Debes decir la verdad.`

### Debería for softer advice

`debería + infinitive`

Example: `Deberías estudiar más.`

### Deber de for probability

`deber de + infinitive`

Example: `Debe de llegar pronto.`

### Negative tener que

`no + tener que + infinitive`

Example: `No tienes que preocuparte.` = There is no obligation to worry.

## 📝 Examples

### Tener que

- `Tengo que estudiar.` — I have to study.
- `Tengo que trabajar.` — I have to work.
- `Tienes que ir.` — You have to go.
- `Tenemos que comprar.` — We have to buy.
- `Tienen que decidir.` — They have to decide.
- `Tuve que salir.` — I had to leave.
- `Tendré que llamar.` — I will have to call.
- `No tienes que preocuparte.` — You do not have to worry.
- `Tengo que estudiar para el examen.` — I have to study for the exam.
- `Tienes que usar el cinturón de seguridad.` — You have to wear a seatbelt.
- `Tenemos que irnos ahora.` — We have to leave now.
- `Tengo que llamar a mi madre.` — I have to call my mother.
- `Tiene que limpiar su habitación.` — He/She has to clean his/her room.
- `Tenemos que terminar el proyecto hoy.` — We have to finish the project today.

### Deber

- `Debes decir la verdad.` — You must tell the truth.
- `Debemos ayudar a los necesitados.` — We should help those in need.
- `Debes comportarte adecuadamente en la reunión.` — You must behave appropriately in the meeting.
- `Debemos respetar las normas.` — We should respect the rules.
- `Debo ayudar en casa.` — I must help at home.
- `Debes hacer tu tarea.` — You must do your homework.
- `Debo llamar a mi abuela.` — I must call my grandmother.
- `Debo mostrar más iniciativa.` — I should show more initiative.
- `Debo llamar a mis padres esta noche.` — I should call my parents tonight.
- `Debes terminar tus tareas antes de salir.` — You should finish your homework before going out.

### Debería

- `Deberías estudiar más.` — You should study more.
- `Deberíamos salir temprano.` — We ought to leave early.
- `Debería ayudar en casa.` — I should help at home.
- `Deberías hacer tu tarea.` — You should do your homework.
- `Deberías estudiar para el examen.` — You should study for the exam.

### Deber de

- `Debe de estar cansado después de tanto trabajo.` — He must be tired after so much work.
- `Debe de llegar pronto.` — He/She is probably arriving soon.
- `Debe de ser muy tarde.` — It must be very late.

### Other source examples

- `Tengo que estudiar para el examen mañana.` — I have to study for the exam tomorrow.
- `Ella tiene que ir al trabajo temprano.` — She has to go to work early.
- `Tengo que asistir a la reunión.` — I have to attend the meeting.
- `Tienes que leer este libro.` — You have to read this book.
- `Debes analizar el texto a fondo.` — You should analyze the text thoroughly.

## ⚠️ Important Notes

- **Tener que** is especially common in everyday conversation and is direct and natural in both informal and formal situations when stating a requirement.
- **Deber** is also used in everyday speech. It can sound more formal, serious, or authoritative when expressing duty, but the source says the choice depends more on **meaning and nuance** than on formality alone.
- Both `tener que` and `deber` can refer to future actions, including intentions or plans. They add a sense of necessity, duty, or advice rather than simply expressing future action.
- The conditional `debería` is commonly used for advice and is not necessarily formal.
- The verb **tener** changes according to the subject; the following infinitive stays in the infinitive.

## 🔄 Conjugation / Forms

The source gives these forms of `deber` in present/conditional use:

| Form | Function shown in source |
|---|---|
| `debo` | duty / obligation |
| `debes` | duty / obligation |
| `debe` | duty / probability when used as `debe de` |
| `debemos` | duty / obligation |
| `deberías` | advice |
| `deberíamos` | advice |
| `debería` | advice |

Examples of `tener` used in the construction:

| Subject | Example form |
|---|---|
| yo | `tengo que` |
| tú | `tienes que` |
| él/ella/usted | `tiene que` |
| nosotros/nosotras | `tenemos que` |
| ellos/ellas/ustedes | `tienen que` |

## 💡 Remember

> **Tener que** = necessity/requirement. **Deber** = duty/responsibility or advice. **Debería** = softer advice. **Deber de** = probability.

> The source emphasizes that context, not a fixed strength scale, determines the nuance.
