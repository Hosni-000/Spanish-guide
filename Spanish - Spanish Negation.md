# Spanish Negation

## 📌 Grammar Rule

Spanish uses negative words to deny or limit an action, event, person, thing, or possibility.

The most common negative words shown in the source are:

- `no` — not
- `nunca` — never
- `jamás` — never / ever
- `nadie` — nobody / no one
- `nada` — nothing / anything in a negative sentence
- `ningún` — no / not any before a masculine singular noun
- `ni` — nor / not even in certain expressions
- `tampoco` — neither / not either

## 🧩 Structure

### Basic negative sentence

`subject + no + conjugated verb + rest of sentence`

Example: `Yo no entiendo.`

The subject is often omitted because the verb ending identifies it:

`no + conjugated verb`

### Negative word after the verb

`no + verb + negative word`

Examples:

- `No veo nada.`
- `No hay nadie aquí.`
- `No tengo ningún problema.`

### Negative word before the verb

A negative word before the verb normally makes an additional `no` unnecessary:

`negative word + verb`

Examples:

- `Nadie vino.`
- `Nunca voy allí.`
- `Ni siquiera llamó.`

### Negative commands and pronouns

- Affirmative command: pronoun attaches to the verb: `Háblame.`
- Negative command: pronoun comes before the verb: `No me hables.`

## 📝 Examples

### Negative words

- `No come carne.` — He/She does not eat meat.
- `Nunca he estado allí.` — I have never been there.
- `Nadie sabe la respuesta.` — No one knows the answer.
- `No hay nada aquí.` — There is nothing here.
- `Juan no trabaja los fines de semana tampoco.` — Juan does not work on weekends either.
- `No me gusta el café. Tampoco me gusta el té.` — I do not like coffee. I do not like tea either.

### Negative concord

- `No veo a nadie.` — I do not see anyone.
- `Nunca digo nada.` — I never say anything.
- `No tengo ningún problema.` — I do not have any problem.
- `Nadie vino.` — Nobody came.
- `Nunca he estado allí.` — I have never been there.

### Negation and verb tense

- Present: `Yo no hablo.` — I do not speak.
- Past: `Él no comió.` — He did not eat.
- Future: `Nosotros no iremos allí.` — We will not live there.
- Compound: `Yo no he terminado.` — I have not finished.
- Verb phrase: `Ella no puede venir.` — She cannot come.

### Negation and pronouns

- `No lo tengo.` — I do not have it.
- `No le hablo.` — I do not talk to him or her.
- `No veo nada.` — I do not see anything.
- `Nadie dijo nada.` — Nobody said anything.
- `Háblame.` — Talk to me.
- `No me hables.` — Do not talk to me.
- `Dímelo.` — Tell it to me.
- `No me lo digas.` — Do not tell it to me.

### More source examples

- `No me gusta el café.` — I don't like coffee.
- `No entiendo la pregunta.` — I do not understand the question.
- `No te preocupes por eso.` — Don't worry about that.

- `No tengo más preguntas.` — I have no further questions.

- `No quiero ir al cine hoy.` — I don't want to go to the movies today.

### Contrast examples from the source

- `Yo tengo dinero.` — I have money.
- `Yo no tengo dinero.` — I do not have money.
- `Él es feliz.` — He is happy.
- `Él no es feliz.` — He is not happy.

## ⚠️ Important Notes

- Spanish uses **negative concord**: multiple negative words can work together to express one negative meaning.
- A postverbal `nadie`, `nada`, or `ningún` normally appears with `no`.
- A negative word placed before the verb normally replaces `no`.
- `nadie` refers to people; `nada` refers to things or actions.
- Use `ningún` before a masculine singular noun; related forms include `ninguna`, `ninguno`, and `ningunos`.
- Negation does **not** change the verb's tense, person, or conjugation ending.
- The source notes that negation alone does not automatically require the subjunctive. It gives `No pienso que él sea culpable.` as an example where the larger construction requires it.

### Incorrect / correct patterns from the source

| Incorrect or less suitable | Correct |
|---|---|
| `Yo no necesito nada nunca` | `Yo nunca necesito nada.` — I never need anything. |
| `No hay nadie aquí no.` | `No hay nadie aquí.` — There is nobody here. |
| `No él va venir` | `Él no va a venir.` — He is not going to come. |
| `Yo no pienso que él es culpable.` | `Yo no pienso que él sea culpable.` — I do not think he is guilty. |
| `Nadie no sabe` | `Nadie sabe` or `No sabe nadie`. |

## 🔄 Conjugation / Forms

Negation uses the existing verb conjugation; it does not create a new verb form.

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| `no + verb` | `No hablo.` |
| `no + verb + nada` | `No veo nada.` |
| `no + verb + nadie` | `No veo a nadie.` |
| `no + verb + ningún + noun` | `No tengo ningún problema.` |
| `nunca + verb` | `Nunca voy allí.` |
| `nadie + verb` | `Nadie vino.` |

## 💡 Remember

> ==Spanish negative words can reinforce one another. `No veo a nadie` is grammatically correct negative concord, not a cancellation into a positive meaning.==
