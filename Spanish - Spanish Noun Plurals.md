# Spanish Noun Plurals

## 📌 Grammar Rule

Spanish noun plurals are usually formed according to the noun's final letter or sound. Articles, adjectives, and other words also show plural agreement.

## 🧩 Structure

### Basic patterns

- `unstressed vowel + s`
- `consonant + es`
- `z -> c + es`

Examples:

- `casa -> casas`
- `animal -> animales`
- `luz -> luces`

## 📝 Examples

### Nouns ending in vowels

- `La casa es grande. Las casas son grandes.` — The house is big. The houses are big.
- `El perro corre. Los perros corren.` — The dog runs. The dogs run.
- `El rubí brilla. Los rubíes brillan.` — The ruby shines. The rubies shine.
- `El tabú es antiguo. Los tabúes son antiguos.` — The taboo is old. The taboos are old.
- `El café está caliente. Los cafés están calientes.` — The coffee is hot. The coffees are hot.

### Nouns ending in consonants

- `El animal duerme. Los animales duermen.` — The animal is sleeping. The animals are sleeping.
- `El reloj funciona. Los relojes funcionan.` — The watch works. The watches work.
- `La mujer sonríe. Las mujeres sonríen.` — The woman smiles. The women smile.

### Nouns ending in -z

Replace `z` with `c` and add `-es`:

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| `pez` | `peces` |
| `voz` | `voces` |
| `luz` | `luces` |
| `lápiz` | `lápices` |

Examples:

- `El pez nada. Los peces nadan.` — The fish swims. The fish swim.
- `La voz es clara. Las voces son claras.` — The voice is clear. The voices are clear.
- `La luz está encendida. Las luces están encendidas.` — The light is on. The lights are on.
- `El lápiz está en la mesa. Los lápices están en la mesa.` — The pencil is on the table. The pencils are on the table.

### Nouns ending in unstressed -s or -x

These generally do **not** change in the plural; the article and other words indicate the number.

- `El lunes es festivo. Los lunes son festivos.` — Monday is a holiday. Mondays are holidays.
- `El tórax está sano. Los tórax están sanos.` — The thorax is healthy. The thoraxes are healthy.

### Stressed final vowels / -s

The source notes that nouns ending in a stressed vowel commonly add `-es` in some patterns, while nouns ending in stressed `-á`, `-é`, or `-ó` normally add `-s` in its examples.

> Some details vary by ending and established usage; the source advises checking a dictionary for irregular or variable plurals.

### Compound nouns

Many compounds made from a verb and a noun are **invariable**:

- `el lavaplatos -> los lavaplatos`
- `el paraguas -> los paraguas`
- `el sacapuntas -> los sacapuntas`

Other compounds pluralize normally:

- `el ferrocarril -> los ferrocarriles`
- `el baloncesto -> los baloncestos`

Some are invariable in form even when singular/plural in meaning:

- `el rascacielos -> los rascacielos`
- `mi cumpleaños -> mis cumpleaños`

### Articles in the plural

- `el -> los`
- `la -> las`
- `un -> unos`
- `una -> unas`

A mixed-gender group is conventionally described with the masculine plural in the source:

- `Dos gatos y dos gatas son cuatro gatos.` — Two male cats and two female cats are four cats.

## ⚠️ Important Notes

- The general plural rules have exceptions and special cases.
- Foreign words and abbreviations may remain unchanged depending on usage.
- Examples from the source include `góspel` and `CD` as forms that may remain unchanged.
- Compound nouns can be variable or invariable; learn established plural forms when necessary.

## 🔄 Conjugation / Forms

| Singular ending / type | Common plural formation |
|---|---|
| unstressed `-a`, `-e`, `-o` | add `-s` |
| most consonants | add `-es` |
| `-z` | change `z -> c` and add `-es` |
| unstressed `-s`, `-x` | usually unchanged |
| some compounds | unchanged or pluralized according to established usage |

## 💡 Remember

> ==Check the final letter/sound of the noun, then verify whether it is one of the special cases such as `-z`, unstressed `-s/-x`, or an invariable compound.==
