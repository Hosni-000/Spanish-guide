# Personal Pronouns

## 📌 Grammar Rule

Spanish personal pronouns vary according to their **grammatical function, person, number, and sometimes gender**.

The source distinguishes:

- **Subject pronouns**: subjects of verbs.
- **Object pronouns**: direct or indirect objects.
- **Possessive forms**: indicate ownership.
- **Reflexive pronouns**: show that the subject performs the action on themself or themselves.

Because Spanish verb endings often identify the subject, subject pronouns are frequently omitted when the meaning is clear.

## 🧩 Structure

### Subject pronouns

`subject pronoun + conjugated verb`

But the pronoun can be omitted when clear:

`conjugated verb`

### Object pronouns

They normally come before a conjugated verb:

`object pronoun + conjugated verb`

They can attach to an infinitive:

`infinitive + object pronoun`

When both indirect and direct object pronouns occur together:

`indirect object pronoun + direct object pronoun + verb`

and **le/les -> se** before `lo`, `la`, `los`, or `las`.

## 📝 Examples

### Subject pronouns

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| First | `yo` | `nosotros / nosotras` |
| Second, informal | `tú` | `vosotros / vosotras` (mainly Spain) |
| Second, formal | `usted` | `ustedes` |
| Third | `él / ella` | `ellos / ellas` |

Examples:

- `Yo soy estudiante.` — I am a student.
- `Tú eres mi amigo.` — You are my friend.
- `Usted es el profesor.` — You are the teacher.
- `Él es médico.` — He is a doctor.
- `Nosotros somos vecinos.` — We are neighbors.
- `Vosotros vais al parque.` — You all are going to the park.
- `Ustedes trabajan aquí.` — You all work here.
- `Ellos estudian español.` — They study Spanish.

### Object pronouns

| Form | Function / source gloss |
|---|---|
| `me` | me |
| `te` | you, singular informal |
| `lo / la` | him, it, or her as a direct object |
| `le` | to him, to her, or to you as an indirect object |
| `nos` | us |
| `os` | you all, informal, mainly Spain |
| `los / las` | them as a direct object |
| `les` | to them or to you all as an indirect object |

Examples:

- `La veo.` — I see her.
- `Voy a verla.` — I am going to see her.
- `Le doy un regalo.` — I give him, her, or you a gift.
- `Voy a darle un regalo.` — I am going to give him, her, or you a gift.
- `Se lo doy.` — I give it to him, her, or you.
- `Vi a Juan y le di el libro.` — I saw John and gave him the book.

### Pronoun attachment

Object and reflexive pronouns can attach to:

- affirmative commands
- gerunds
- infinitives

An accent mark may be required to preserve the original stress when a pronoun attaches to a gerund or command.

Examples:

- `Dámelo.` — Give it to me.
- `Estoy haciéndolo.` — I am doing it.
- `Necesito comprarlo.` — I need to buy it.

### Reflexive pronouns

- `Me lavo.` — I wash myself.
- `Voy a lavarme.` — I am going to wash myself.
- `Estoy lavándome.` — I am washing myself.

## ⚠️ Important Notes

- Subject pronouns are often omitted when the verb ending makes the subject clear.
- A speaker may include a subject pronoun for contrast, emphasis, or extra clarity.
- Object and possessive forms have different functions and should not be confused with subject pronouns.
- When direct and indirect object pronouns appear together, the indirect object pronoun comes first.
- `le` or `les` changes to `se` before `lo`, `la`, `los`, or `las`.
- Pronouns are positioned differently with affirmative and negative commands.
- `Vosotros/vosotras` is used mainly in Spain; most of Latin America uses `ustedes` for plural “you.”

## 🔄 Conjugation / Forms

### Common Spanish subject pronouns

| English | Spanish |
|---|---|
| I | `yo` |
| you, singular informal | `tú` |
| you, singular formal | `usted` |
| he | `él` |
| she | `ella` |
| we | `nosotros / nosotras` |
| you, plural informal, mainly Spain | `vosotros / vosotras` |
| you, plural | `ustedes` |
| they | `ellos / ellas` |

### Common possessive forms shown with personal pronouns

| English | Spanish |
|---|---|
| mine | `mío / mía` |
| yours, singular informal | `tuyo / tuya` |
| his/hers/yours formal/theirs | `suyo / suya` |
| ours | `nuestro / nuestra` |
| yours, plural informal, mainly Spain | `vuestro / vuestra` |

## 💡 Remember

> Spanish often omits the subject pronoun because the **verb ending identifies the subject**. Use the pronoun when the source's contexts call for emphasis, contrast, or clarity.
