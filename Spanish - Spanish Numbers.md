# Spanish Numbers

## 📌 Grammar Rule

The source covers **cardinal, ordinal, multiplicative, collective, fractional, compound, monetary, address, measurement, and date** uses of numbers in Spanish.

## 🧩 Structure

### Cardinal quantities

Use cardinal numbers for counting, amounts, and specific numerical information.

### Compound numbers 21–29

`veinti- + unit` as one word.

### Compound numbers 30–99

`tens + y + unit`

Example: `treinta y uno`.

### Dates

`day + de + month + de + year`

### Fractions

`fraction + de + noun`

### Measurements

`number + unit`

### Collective numbers

`article/determiner + collective noun + de + quantity`

### Cien and ciento

Use **ciento** for numbers from 101 to 199 and, more generally, when another number follows 100:

- `101: ciento uno`
- `102: ciento dos`
- `156: ciento cincuenta y seis`

Use **cien** before a noun and in exact hundreds such as:

- `cien personas`
- `cien mil`

The source gives these irregular hundreds:

- `500: quinientos`
- `700: setecientos`
- `900: novecientos`

### Compound numbers

21–29 are written as one word:

- `21: veintiuno`
- `22: veintidós`
- `23: veintitrés`
- `24: veinticuatro`
- `25: veinticinco`
- `26: veintiséis`
- `27: veintisiete`
- `28: veintiocho`
- `29: veintinueve`

From 30–99, use separate words with `y`:

- `treinta y uno`
- `cuarenta y dos`
- `cincuenta y cinco`
- `sesenta y cinco`
- `setenta y ocho`

Before a feminine noun, `uno` changes to `una`:

- `veintiuna personas`
- `treinta y una páginas`

Examples:

- `Veintidós niños juegan en el parque.` — Twenty-two children are playing in the park.
- `Mi abuela tiene sesenta y cinco años.` — My grandmother is 65 years old.
- `Hay cuarenta y tres estudiantes en la clase.` — There are 43 students in the class.
- `El libro cuesta setenta y ocho pesos.` — The book costs 78 pesos.

### Ordinal numbers

Ordinal numbers show position or order. They normally come before the noun and agree in gender and number.

| Number | Ordinal |
|---:|---|
| 1 | `primero/a` |
| 2 | `segundo/a` |
| 3 | `tercero/a` |
| 4 | `cuarto/a` |
| 5 | `quinto/a` |
| 6 | `sexto/a` |
| 7 | `séptimo/a` |
| 8 | `octavo/a` |
| 9 | `noveno/a` |
| 10 | `décimo/a` |

Before a masculine singular noun:

- `primero -> primer`
- `tercero -> tercer`

Examples:

- `El primer capítulo.` — The first chapter.
- `El tercer día.` — The third day.
- `La primera vez.` — The first time.
- `Quedó en segundo lugar.` — He/She came in second place.
- `Vivo en la tercera casa.` — I live in the third house.
- `Es el cuarto día.` — It is the fourth day.
- `La quinta avenida está cerca.` — Fifth Avenue is nearby.
- `El sexto sentido.` — The sixth sense.
- `La séptima maravilla.` — The seventh wonder.
- `Vivo en el octavo piso.` — I live on the eighth floor.
- `Está en noveno grado.` — He/She is in ninth grade.
- `Es la décima vez.` — It is the tenth time.

### Multiplicative numbers

Multiplicative numbers express how many times an amount is multiplied. They can function as adjectives or nouns and agree in gender and number when they modify a noun.

| Number | Spanish |
|---:|---|
| 2 | `doble` |
| 3 | `triple` |
| 4 | `cuádruple` |
| 5 | `quíntuple` |
| 6 | `séxtuple` |
| 7 | `séptuple` |
| 8 | `óctuple` |
| 9 | `nónuplo` |
| 10 | `décuple` |

Examples:

- `El resultado es el doble.` — The result is double.
- `Necesitamos una cantidad triple.` — We need a quantity three times as large.
- `Ganaron un premio cuádruple.` — They won a quadruple prize.
- `El nacimiento quíntuple fue noticia.` — The quintuple birth was news.
- `La campeona logró una victoria séxtuple.` — The champion achieved a sixfold victory.
- `El aumento fue séptuple.` — The increase was sevenfold.
- `El crecimiento fue óctuple.` — The growth was eightfold.
- `La producción fue nónupla.` — Production was ninefold.
- `La distancia es décuple.` — The distance is ten times as great.

> The source says these forms are relatively formal; everyday speech often uses expressions such as `tres veces más` or `el doble de`.

### Collective numbers

Collective terms name a group or quantity as a single unit. The source treats them as nouns, so they normally occur with an article or another determiner.

| Spanish | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `docena` | a dozen / 12 units |
| `decena` | a group of ten |
| `centena` | a hundred |
| `millar` | a thousand |
| `quincena` | a period of 15 days / a fortnight |

Examples:

- `Compré una docena de huevos.` — I bought a dozen eggs.
- `La biblioteca tiene más de una centena de libros raros.` — The library has more than a hundred rare books.
- `Hay un millar de estrellas visibles en el cielo esta noche.` — There are a thousand visible stars in the sky tonight.
- `El equipo ha ganado una decena de premios este año.` — The team has won ten awards this year.
- `Tendré vacaciones durante la quincena del festival.` — I will have a two-week vacation during the festival period.

### Fractions

Common fractions:

- `un medio` — one half (1/2)
- `un tercio` — one third (1/3)
- `un cuarto` — one quarter (1/4)
- `dos tercios` — two thirds (2/3)
- `tres cuartos` — three quarters (3/4)

The denominator normally appears as a masculine noun. The numerator agrees in number:

- `un tercio`
- `dos tercios`

Fractions commonly express a portion with `de`:

- `Compró medio kilo de queso.` — He/She bought half a kilo of cheese.
- `La receta requiere un cuarto de taza de azúcar.` — The recipe requires a quarter cup of sugar.
- `Bebí tres cuartos de la botella.` — I drank three quarters of the bottle.
- `Leímos dos tercios del libro.` — We read two thirds of the book.

### Money

The source says the decimal comma is standard in most Spanish-speaking contexts. A period or a space may separate thousands.

| English-style notation | Common Spanish notation |
|---|---|
| `$1,234.56` | `1.234,56 $` |
| `€2,000.75` | `2.000,75 €` |
| `$5.00` | `5,00 $` |

Example:

`Cuesta mil doscientos treinta y cuatro dólares con cincuenta y seis centavos.` — It costs 1,234.56 dollars.

In international or technical contexts, the source gives `1 234,56 EUR` as an example.

### Addresses

Spanish addresses commonly use cardinal numbers for street, building, and apartment numbers. The format varies by country/style.

- `Calle Mayor, 123.` — 123 Calle Mayor.
- `123 Calle Mayor.` — 123 Calle Mayor.
- `3.° piso.` — third floor.
- `2.ª planta.` — second floor.
- `3.° piso, apartamento 5.` — third floor, apartment 5.
- `2.ª planta, puerta 3.` — second floor, apartment 3.

### Measurements

- `una tonelada` — one ton
- `dos litros` — two liters
- `cinco metros` — five meters
- `veintiuna libras` — twenty-one pounds

Decimal separator:

- `3,5 litros` — 3.5 liters
- `2,75 metros` — 2.75 meters

The source prefers a space between number and unit in scientific/technical writing:

- `15 kg`
- `100 ml`

Fractions:

- `medio litro` — half a liter
- `tres cuartos de kilo` — three quarters of a kilogram

### Dates with numbers

- `El 5 de octubre de 2021.` — October 5, 2021.
- `El 15 de octubre.` — October 15.
- `El primero de octubre empieza el curso.` — The course starts on October 1.
- `La reunión es el 2 de noviembre de 2023.` — The meeting is on November 2, 2023.

## ⚠️ Important Notes

- `uno` changes to `una` before a feminine noun in the source's examples: `veintiuna personas`, `treinta y una páginas`.
- For the first day of a month, both `el 1 de...` and `el primero de...` are used.
- Ordinal `primero` and `tercero` become `primer` and `tercer` before masculine singular nouns.
- Number + unit normally uses singular after `uno` and plural after other numbers.
- The numeral `uno` agrees with a feminine unit; the source also applies this to `veintiuno` and compound forms ending in `uno`.
- In Spanish monetary notation, decimal comma usage and the placement of currency symbols vary by country/style.
- The source says address formats vary by country.

## 🔄 Conjugation / Forms

Numbers do not conjugate. The key form changes shown in the source are:

| Context | Form |
|---|---|
| exact 100 | `cien` |
| 101+ when another number follows | `ciento...` |
| before feminine noun | `uno -> una` |
| masculine singular ordinal | `primero -> primer`, `tercero -> tercer` |

## 💡 Remember

> ==For compound numbers, 21–29 are one word (`veintidós`), while 30–99 use `tens + y + unit` (`treinta y dos`).==
