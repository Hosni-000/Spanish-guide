# Tener Idiomatic Expressions

## 📌 Grammar Rule

The verb **tener** primarily means “to have,” but Spanish also uses it in many fixed expressions whose meanings do not translate literally.

In these expressions, **tener + noun** can describe physical sensations, feelings, emotions, circumstances, or other states. English often uses **to be** for the same ideas.

> ==Conjugate `tener` according to the subject in all of these patterns.==

## 🧩 Structure

### Fixed expressions with tener

`tener + noun`

Examples: `tener hambre`, `tener sed`, `tener miedo`

### Obligation

`tener (conjugated) + que + infinitive`

Example: `tengo que estudiar`

### Age

`tener + number + años`

Example: `tener 20 años`

Spanish expresses age with **tener**, not **ser**.

| `tener frío` | to be cold |

### Source examples

- `Tengo hambre.` — I am hungry.
- `Tengo sed.` — I am thirsty.
- `Tengo frío.` — I am cold.
- `Tengo calor.` — I am hot.
- `Tengo suerte.` — I am lucky.
- `Tengo prisa.` — I am in a hurry.
- `Tengo miedo.` — I am afraid.
- `Tienes razón.` — You are right.
- `Ten cuidado.` — Be careful.
- `Tengo éxito.` — I am successful.
- `Tener 20 años.` — To be 20 years old.
- `Tener 30 años.` — To be 30 years old.

### More source examples

- `Tienes razón en este caso.` — You are right in this case.
- `Tenemos sed después del ejercicio.` — We are thirsty after exercise.
- `Tienes ganas de bailar.` — You feel like dancing.
- `Tenemos calor en verano.` — We are hot in the summer.
- `Tengo cuidado al cruzar la calle.` — I am careful when crossing the street.
- `Tienes sueño por la noche.` — You are sleepy at night.
- `Tiene suerte en los juegos.` — He/She is lucky in games.
- `Tienen frío en la montaña.` — They are cold in the mountain.
- `Tenemos miedo a las alturas.` — We are afraid of heights.
- `Tengo dolor de cabeza.` — I have a headache.
- `Tengo prisa para llegar a tiempo.` — I am in a rush to be on time.
- `Tienes fiebre hoy.` — You have a fever today.
- `Tiene fiebre desde ayer.` — He has had a fever since yesterday.
- `Tienes sed y necesitas beber.` — You are thirsty and need to drink.
- `Tienes cuidado al manejar.` — You are careful when driving.
- `Tiene calor en la playa.` — He/She is hot at the beach.
- `Tiene frío en invierno.` — He is cold in winter.
- `Tengo suerte en el amor.` — I am lucky in love.
- `Tienen envidia de su talento.` — They are envious of his talent.
- `Tiene hambre por la mañana.` — He/She is hungry in the morning.

### Tener as possession

- `Tengo un libro.` — I have a book.
- `Él tiene una casa.` — He has a house.

## ⚠️ Important Notes

- The meaning of `tener` depends on the expression in fixed phrases.
- Spanish uses **tener** where English often uses **to be** for sensations and states such as hunger, thirst, heat, cold, sleepiness, fear, and luck.
- The source describes these as **expresiones idiomáticas con tener** and notes that learning the phrases as fixed expressions is important.

## 🔄 Conjugation / Forms

The source demonstrates that `tener` must agree with the subject, but the main focus of these lessons is the fixed expression rather than a full conjugation table.

## 💡 Remember

> Do not translate these expressions word-for-word. Learn `tener + noun` as a complete Spanish phrase.
