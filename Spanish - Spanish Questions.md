# Spanish Questions

## 📌 Grammar Rule

The source identifies three useful types of questions in Spanish:

1. **Yes/no questions**: normally invite a `sí` or `no` answer.
2. **Open-ended questions**: request specific information and commonly begin with an interrogative word or phrase.
3. **Tag questions**: add a short tag to seek confirmation or agreement.

Spanish does **not** normally require subject-verb inversion to form a question.

## 🧩 Structure

### Yes/no questions

A statement can become a question through **question marks and intonation** without changing the verb form.

`¿statement word order?`

The subject may remain before the verb or follow the verb:

- `¿Ella come manzanas?`
- `¿Come ella manzanas?`

### Open-ended questions

`interrogative word/phrase + flexible word order`

### Tag questions

`statement + short tag`

Common tags: `¿verdad?`, `¿no?`, `¿cierto?`, `¿no es así?`

## 📝 Examples

### Yes/no questions

- `¿Tienes hambre?` — Are you hungry?
- `¿Vienes con nosotros?` — Are you coming with us?
- `¿Ella come manzanas?` — Does she eat apples?
- `¿Come ella manzanas?` — Does she eat apples?
- `¿Escribes tú cartas?` — Do you write letters?
- `¿Tú vienes?` — Are you coming?
- `¿Tú quieres salir?` — Do you want to go out?
- `¿Estás seguro?` — Are you sure?

### Open-ended questions

| Interrogative | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `¿Qué?` | What? |
| `¿Quién? / ¿Quiénes?` | Who? |
| `¿Dónde?` | Where? |
| `¿Cuándo?` | When? |
| `¿Por qué?` | Why? |
| `¿Cómo?` | How? |
| `¿Cuánto / cuánta / cuántos / cuántas?` | How much? / How many? |

Examples:

- `¿Qué haces?` — What are you doing?
- `¿Cómo te llamas?` — What is your name?
- `¿Dónde vives?` — Where do you live?
- `¿Dónde estás?` — Where are you?
- `¿Por qué estudias español?` — Why are you studying Spanish?
- `¿Cuántos libros tienes?` — How many books do you have?
- `¿Dónde está el baño?` — Where is the bathroom?
- `¿Qué hora es?` — What time is it?
- `¿Cómo estás?` — How are you?
- `¿Cuánto cuesta esto?` — How much does this cost?
- `¿Qué necesitas?` — What do you need?
- `¿Cuándo nos vemos?` — When do we meet?
- `¿Qué quieres comer?` — What do you want to eat?
- `¿Qué opinas?` — What do you think?
- `¿Qué estudias?` — What do you study?
- `¿Cuál es tu color favorito?` — What is your favorite color?
- `¿Cuándo sale el tren?` — When does the train leave?
- `¿Cuándo es la fiesta?` — When is the party?

### Tag questions

- `Estás cansado, ¿verdad?` — You are tired, right?
- `Es tu hermano, ¿no?` — He is your brother, right?
- `Hace mucho calor hoy, ¿verdad?` — It's very hot today, right?
- `Tú eres mi amigo, ¿no?` — You're my friend, right?
- `Estamos listos para irnos, ¿cierto?` — We're ready to leave, right?
- `Ella es la nueva profesora, ¿no es así?` — She is the new teacher, isn't she?
- `Vienes a la fiesta, ¿verdad?` — You're coming to the party, right?
- `No te gusta el café, ¿no?` — You do not like coffee, right?

## ⚠️ Important Notes

- Direct questions use **opening `¿` and closing `?`** in writing.
- In yes/no questions, the subject may stay before the verb or follow it.
- Open-ended question word order is flexible; the source says not to assume one fixed order.
- Interrogative words carry an **accent mark** in direct questions.
- `Cuanto` agrees in gender and number with the noun when it modifies one: `cuánto`, `cuánta`, `cuántos`, `cuántas`.
- Tag questions are generally **invariable**: the tag does not change with the gender or number of the person addressed.
- Intonation can affect emphasis and nuance. The source notes that a yes/no question commonly has rising pitch toward the end, but exact intonation varies by region and context.
- Stress can highlight contrast or express surprise, but the words alone do not determine one fixed interpretation.

## 🔄 Conjugation / Forms

No special question conjugation is required for the question types described in the source.

## 💡 Remember

> Spanish questions usually depend on **question marks, interrogative words, word order, and intonation**, not on a special auxiliary verb or mandatory subject-verb inversion.
