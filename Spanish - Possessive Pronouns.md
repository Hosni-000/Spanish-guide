# Possessive Pronouns

## 📌 Grammar Rule

Spanish **possessive pronouns** replace a noun phrase and indicate ownership or association.

They agree in **gender and number with the noun they replace**, not with the possessor.

> ==The noun being replaced determines the possessive pronoun's gender and number.==

The source distinguishes **unstressed possessive adjectives** (`mi`, `tu`, `su`, etc.) from **stressed possessive forms** (`mío`, `tuyo`, `suyo`, etc.).

## 🧩 Structure

### Possessive adjective before a noun

`possessive adjective + noun`

Examples: `mi casa`, `tus libros`, `nuestra mesa`

### Stressed possessive pronoun replacing a noun phrase

`definite article + stressed possessive`

Examples: `el mío`, `la tuya`, `los nuestros`, `las suyas`

### After ser

The stressed possessive normally appears **without an article**:

`noun + ser + stressed possessive`

Example: `El libro es mío.`

## 📝 Examples

### Stressed possessive forms

| Possessor | Masculine singular | Feminine singular | Masculine plural | Feminine plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mine | `mío` | `mía` | `míos` | `mías` |
| yours, informal singular | `tuyo` | `tuya` | `tuyos` | `tuyas` |
| his/hers/yours formal singular/theirs | `suyo` | `suya` | `suyos` | `suyas` |
| ours | `nuestro` | `nuestra` | `nuestros` | `nuestras` |
| yours, informal plural in Spain | `vuestro` | `vuestra` | `vuestros` | `vuestras` |

### Unstressed possessive adjectives

| Number / form | Forms | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Singular | `mi`, `tu`, `su` | `mi perro`, `tu libro`, `su coche` |
| Plural | `mis`, `tus`, `sus` | `mis perros`, `tus libros`, `sus coches` |
| Masculine with nuestro/vuestro | `nuestro`, `vuestro` | `nuestro amigo`, `vuestro libro` |
| Feminine with nuestro/vuestro | `nuestra`, `vuestra` | `nuestra amiga`, `vuestra mesa` |

### Replacing a noun phrase

- `Es mi libro. Es mío.` — It is my book. It is mine.
- `Este libro es mío.` — This book is mine.
- `La casa grande es nuestra.` — The big house is ours.
- `Tus zapatos son más cómodos que los míos.` — Your shoes are more comfortable than mine.
- `Mis amigos son divertidos. ¿Cómo son los tuyos?` — My friends are fun. What are yours like?
- `El coche es suyo.` — The car is his, hers, yours, or theirs.
- `Estos problemas son nuestros.` — These problems are ours.
- `¿De quién es esta mochila? Es mía.` — Whose backpack is this? It is mine.
- `¿Son tus llaves? No, son suyas.` — Are these your keys? No, they are his, hers, yours, or theirs.

### Gender and number agreement

- `El libro es mío.` — The book is mine.
- `Los libros son míos.` — The books are mine.
- `La casa es tuya.` — The house is yours.
- `Las casas son tuyas.` — The houses are yours.
- `El coche es nuestro.` — The car is ours.
- `La casa es nuestra.` — The house is ours.
- `El libro es vuestro.` — The book is yours (informal plural, in Spain).
- `Las mesas son vuestras.` — The tables are yours (informal plural, in Spain).

### Stressed vs. unstressed forms

Unstressed forms:

- `mi casa` — my house
- `tus libros` — your books
- `nuestras sillas` — our chairs

Stressed forms:

- `El libro es mío.` — The book is mine.
- `La casa tuya es más grande.` — Your house is bigger.
- `Los libros suyos están aquí.` — His, hers, yours, or their books are here.
- `Esa es la mochila tuya, no la mía.` — That is your backpack, not mine.

### Formal and informal examples

- `¿Es su casa, señor?` — Is it your house, sir?
- `¿La casa es suya?` — Is the house yours?
- `¿Es tu casa?` — Is it your house?
- `¿La casa es tuya?` — Is the house yours?

## ⚠️ Important Notes

- **Unstressed** forms come before the noun: `mi`, `tu`, `su`, `nuestro`, `vuestro`, etc.
- **Stressed** forms can stand alone, commonly with a definite article, or can follow a noun for emphasis or contrast.
- When a stressed possessive replaces a noun phrase, the definite article agrees with the omitted noun: `el mío`, `la tuya`, `los nuestros`, `las suyas`.
- Do **not** use an article before an unstressed possessive adjective directly before a noun in standard Spanish: `mi libro`, not `el mi libro`.
- After **ser**, the stressed possessive normally appears without an article: `El libro es mío.`
- `suyo` can be ambiguous: it may mean his, hers, yours (formal), or theirs. Context or an added phrase such as `de él` or `de ellos` may be needed.
- For plural `ustedes`, regional usage matters. In most of Latin America, `ustedes` is used for both formal and informal plural address, so `su` and `suyo` serve both contexts. In Spain, `vosotros` normally takes `vuestro`, while formal plural `ustedes` takes `su`.

### Common mistakes from the source

- Incorrect agreement: `El mío` cannot replace `la casa`; the correct form is `la mía`.
- Wrong article construction: `Quiero el mío.` but `El libro es mío.`
- Confusing adjective and pronoun: `Es mi libro. Es mío.`
- Wrong gender/number: `Las casas son mías`, not `Las casas son mío.`
- Ambiguous `suyo`: `El coche es suyo, el de Ana.`

## 🔄 Conjugation / Forms

### Pronoun forms

| Person / meaning | Unstressed adjective | Stressed possessive |
|---|---|---|
| mine | `mi / mis` | `mío, mía, míos, mías` |
| yours, informal singular | `tu / tus` | `tuyo, tuya, tuyos, tuyas` |
| his/hers/yours formal/theirs | `su / sus` | `suyo, suya, suyos, suyas` |
| ours | `nuestro/a/os/as` | `nuestro, nuestra, nuestros, nuestras` |
| yours, informal plural Spain | `vuestro/a/os/as` | `vuestro, vuestra, vuestros, vuestras` |

## 💡 Remember

> **Possessive adjectives** accompany a noun: `mi libro`. **Possessive pronouns** replace the noun: `el mío` / `El libro es mío`.

> The possessive pronoun agrees with the **thing possessed**, not the person who possesses it.
