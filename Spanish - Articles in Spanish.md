# Articles in Spanish

## 📌 Grammar Rule

Spanish has **four definite articles** and **four indefinite articles**, and they agree with the noun in gender and number.

### Definite articles

- `el` — masculine singular
- `la` — feminine singular
- `los` — masculine plural
- `las` — feminine plural

### Indefinite articles

- `un` — masculine singular
- `una` — feminine singular
- `unos` — masculine plural
- `unas` — feminine plural

## 🧩 Structure

### Definite article

`article + noun`

Examples: `el libro`, `la casa`, `los libros`, `las casas`

### Indefinite article

`article + noun`

Examples: `un libro`, `una casa`, `unos libros`, `unas casas`

## 📝 Examples

### Definite articles

- `El libro está aquí.` — The book is here.
- `El perro duerme.` — The dog is sleeping.
- `La casa es grande.` — The house is big.
- `La mesa está limpia.` — The table is clean.
- `Los libros están aquí.` — The books are here.
- `Los perros duermen.` — The dogs are sleeping.
- `Las casas son grandes.` — The houses are big.
- `Las mesas están limpias.` — The tables are clean.
- `El libro que me prestaste es interesante.` — The book you lent me is interesting.
- `La luna está llena.` — The moon is full.
- `La educación es importante.` — Education is important.
- `El lunes empieza el curso.` — The course starts on Monday.
- `El señor Pérez llegó temprano.` — Mr. Pérez arrived early.

### Indefinite articles

- `Un libro está sobre la mesa.` — A book is on the table.
- `Una casa está en venta.` — A house is for sale.
- `Unos amigos vienen esta noche.` — Some friends are coming tonight.
- `Unas manzanas están maduras.` — Some apples are ripe.

## ⚠️ Important Notes

### When to use definite articles

The source lists these uses:

1. Specific or previously mentioned things: `El libro que me prestaste es interesante.`
2. Unique things such as the moon and sun: `La luna está llena.`
3. General categories or abstract concepts: `La educación es importante.`
4. Before days of the week and, commonly, seasons: `El lunes empieza el curso.`
5. Before a title when it is used as a noun phrase rather than direct address: `El señor Pérez llegó temprano.`

### Exceptions / special uses

- **Titles in direct address**: the article is normally omitted.
  - `Señora García, ¿puede ayudarme?` — Mrs. García, can you help me?
  - With the title + name as an ordinary noun phrase, the article is generally used: `La señora García llegó.` — Mrs. García arrived.

- **Days of the week**: Spanish normally uses a definite article.
  - `El lunes tengo una cita.` — I have an appointment on Monday.
  - `Trabajo los viernes.` — I work on Fridays.

- **Abstract nouns**: definite articles are frequently used.
  - `La libertad es importante.` — Freedom is important.
  - `El amor puede cambiar a una persona.` — Love can change a person.

- **Languages**: after `hablar` and `aprender`, the language name is typically used without an article, but an article can be used when the language is treated as a specific subject/object.
  - `Hablo español.` — I speak Spanish.
  - `Estoy aprendiendo el español de México.` — I am learning the Spanish of Mexico.

- **Professions after ser**: a profession is usually used without an indefinite article when it simply identifies someone's occupation. The article is used when the profession is qualified or presented as a specific individual.
  - `Es ingeniero.` — He is an engineer.
  - `Es un ingeniero muy reconocido.` — He is a highly regarded engineer.

- **Preposition + article combinations** shown in the source:
  - `Voy a la ciudad.` — I am going to the city.
  - `Vengo del mercado.` — I am coming from the market.

- **Idiomatic expression**:
  - `¿Me haces el favor?` — Could you do me the favor?

### Mixed-gender groups

For mixed-gender groups, the source says Spanish traditionally uses the masculine plural form `los`.

Example:

`Los niños y las niñas...`

## 🔄 Conjugation / Forms

| Number | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| Singular definite | `el` | `la` |
| Plural definite | `los` | `las` |
| Singular indefinite | `un` | `una` |
| Plural indefinite | `unos` | `unas` |

## 💡 Remember

> ==Articles must agree with the noun in gender and number.==

> Pay special attention to **days of the week, titles in direct address, abstract nouns, language names, professions, and article combinations**.
