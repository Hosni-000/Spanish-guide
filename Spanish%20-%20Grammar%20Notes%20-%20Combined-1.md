# Tener que and Deber

## 📌 Grammar Rule

Spanish uses **tener que** and **deber** to express obligation or necessity.

- **tener que** commonly presents an action as necessary or required, often because of circumstances, practical needs, or an external requirement.
- **deber + infinitive** commonly expresses duty, responsibility, or what is advisable.
- **debería + infinitive** is commonly used for advice or recommendation and is softer than a direct obligation.
- **deber de + infinitive** expresses supposition, likelihood, or probability.

> ==The source stresses that the difference between `tener que` and `deber` is not an absolute difference in strength. Context determines how forceful the expression sounds.==

## 🧩 Structure

### Tener que

`tener (conjugated) + que + infinitive`

Example: `Tengo que estudiar.`

### Deber for obligation or advice

`deber + infinitive`

Example: `Debes decir la verdad.`

### Debería for softer advice

`debería + infinitive`

Example: `Deberías estudiar más.`

### Deber de for probability

`deber de + infinitive`

Example: `Debe de llegar pronto.`

### Negative tener que

`no + tener que + infinitive`

Example: `No tienes que preocuparte.` = There is no obligation to worry.

## 📝 Examples

### Tener que

- `Tengo que estudiar.` — I have to study.
- `Tengo que trabajar.` — I have to work.
- `Tienes que ir.` — You have to go.
- `Tenemos que comprar.` — We have to buy.
- `Tienen que decidir.` — They have to decide.
- `Tuve que salir.` — I had to leave.
- `Tendré que llamar.` — I will have to call.
- `No tienes que preocuparte.` — You do not have to worry.
- `Tengo que estudiar para el examen.` — I have to study for the exam.
- `Tienes que usar el cinturón de seguridad.` — You have to wear a seatbelt.
- `Tenemos que irnos ahora.` — We have to leave now.
- `Tengo que llamar a mi madre.` — I have to call my mother.
- `Tiene que limpiar su habitación.` — He/She has to clean his/her room.
- `Tenemos que terminar el proyecto hoy.` — We have to finish the project today.

### Deber

- `Debes decir la verdad.` — You must tell the truth.
- `Debemos ayudar a los necesitados.` — We should help those in need.
- `Debes comportarte adecuadamente en la reunión.` — You must behave appropriately in the meeting.
- `Debemos respetar las normas.` — We should respect the rules.
- `Debo ayudar en casa.` — I must help at home.
- `Debes hacer tu tarea.` — You must do your homework.
- `Debo llamar a mi abuela.` — I must call my grandmother.
- `Debo mostrar más iniciativa.` — I should show more initiative.
- `Debo llamar a mis padres esta noche.` — I should call my parents tonight.
- `Debes terminar tus tareas antes de salir.` — You should finish your homework before going out.

### Debería

- `Deberías estudiar más.` — You should study more.
- `Deberíamos salir temprano.` — We ought to leave early.
- `Debería ayudar en casa.` — I should help at home.
- `Deberías hacer tu tarea.` — You should do your homework.
- `Deberías estudiar para el examen.` — You should study for the exam.

### Deber de

- `Debe de estar cansado después de tanto trabajo.` — He must be tired after so much work.
- `Debe de llegar pronto.` — He/She is probably arriving soon.
- `Debe de ser muy tarde.` — It must be very late.

### Other source examples

- `Tengo que estudiar para el examen mañana.` — I have to study for the exam tomorrow.
- `Ella tiene que ir al trabajo temprano.` — She has to go to work early.
- `Tengo que asistir a la reunión.` — I have to attend the meeting.
- `Tienes que leer este libro.` — You have to read this book.
- `Debes analizar el texto a fondo.` — You should analyze the text thoroughly.

## ⚠️ Important Notes

- **Tener que** is especially common in everyday conversation and is direct and natural in both informal and formal situations when stating a requirement.
- **Deber** is also used in everyday speech. It can sound more formal, serious, or authoritative when expressing duty, but the source says the choice depends more on **meaning and nuance** than on formality alone.
- Both `tener que` and `deber` can refer to future actions, including intentions or plans. They add a sense of necessity, duty, or advice rather than simply expressing future action.
- The conditional `debería` is commonly used for advice and is not necessarily formal.
- The verb **tener** changes according to the subject; the following infinitive stays in the infinitive.

## 🔄 Conjugation / Forms

The source gives these forms of `deber` in present/conditional use:

| Form | Function shown in source |
|---|---|
| `debo` | duty / obligation |
| `debes` | duty / obligation |
| `debe` | duty / probability when used as `debe de` |
| `debemos` | duty / obligation |
| `deberías` | advice |
| `deberíamos` | advice |
| `debería` | advice |

Examples of `tener` used in the construction:

| Subject | Example form |
|---|---|
| yo | `tengo que` |
| tú | `tienes que` |
| él/ella/usted | `tiene que` |
| nosotros/nosotras | `tenemos que` |
| ellos/ellas/ustedes | `tienen que` |

## 💡 Remember

> **Tener que** = necessity/requirement. **Deber** = duty/responsibility or advice. **Debería** = softer advice. **Deber de** = probability.

> The source emphasizes that context, not a fixed strength scale, determines the nuance.

# Tener Idiomatic Expressions

## 📌 Grammar Rule

The verb **tener** primarily means “to have,” but Spanish also uses it in many fixed expressions whose meanings do not translate literally.

In these expressions, **tener + noun** can describe physical sensations, feelings, emotions, circumstances, or other states. English often uses **to be** for the same ideas.

> ==Conjugate `tener` according to the subject in all of these patterns.==

## 🧩 Structure

### Fixed expressions with tener

`tener + noun`

Examples: `tener hambre`, `tener sed`, `tener miedo`

### Obligation

`tener (conjugated) + que + infinitive`

Example: `tengo que estudiar`

### Age

`tener + number + años`

Example: `tener 20 años`

Spanish expresses age with **tener**, not **ser**.

## 📝 Examples

### Common expressions

| Spanish expression | Meaning in English |
|---|---|
| `tener hambre` | to be hungry |
| `tener sed` | to be thirsty |
| `tener calor` | to be hot |
| `tener frío` | to be cold |
| `tener sueño` | to be sleepy |
| `tener suerte` | to be lucky |
| `tener prisa` | to be in a hurry |
| `tener miedo` | to be afraid |
| `tener razón` | to be right |
| `tener cuidado` | to be careful |
| `tener éxito` | to be successful / to succeed |
| `tener ganas de` | to feel like / to feel like doing something |
| `tener dolor de cabeza` | to have a headache |
| `tener fiebre` | to have a fever |
| `tener envidia de` | to be envious of |

### Source examples

- `Tengo hambre.` — I am hungry.
- `Tengo sed.` — I am thirsty.
- `Tengo frío.` — I am cold.
- `Tengo calor.` — I am hot.

- `Tengo prisa.` — I am in a hurry.
- `Tengo miedo.` — I am afraid.

### More source examples

- `Tienes razón en este caso.` — You are right in this case.
- `Tenemos sed después del ejercicio.` — We are thirsty after exercise.
- `Tienes ganas de bailar.` — You feel like dancing.
- `Tenemos calor en verano.` — We are hot in the summer.
- `Tengo cuidado al cruzar la calle.` — I am careful when crossing the street.
- `Tienes sueño por la noche.` — You are sleepy at night.
- `Tiene suerte en los juegos.` — He/She is lucky in games.
- `Tienen frío en la montaña.` — They are cold in the mountain.
- `Tenemos miedo a las alturas.` — We are afraid of heights.
- `Tengo dolor de cabeza.` — I have a headache.
- `Tengo prisa para llegar a tiempo.` — I am in a rush to be on time.
- `Tienes fiebre hoy.` — You have a fever today.

- `Tienes cuidado al manejar.` — You are careful when driving.

- `Tiene frío en invierno.` — He is cold in winter.
- `Tengo suerte en el amor.` — I am lucky in love.
- `Tienen envidia de su talento.` — They are envious of his talent.
- `Tiene hambre por la mañana.` — He/She is hungry in the morning.

### Tener as possession

- `Tengo un libro.` — I have a book.
- `Él tiene una casa.` — He has a house.

## ⚠️ Important Notes

- The meaning of `tener` depends on the expression in fixed phrases.
- Spanish uses **tener** where English often uses **to be** for sensations and states such as hunger, thirst, heat, cold, sleepiness, fear, and luck.
- The source describes these as **expresiones idiomáticas con tener** and notes that learning the phrases as fixed expressions is important.

## 🔄 Conjugation / Forms

The source demonstrates that `tener` must agree with the subject, but the main focus of these lessons is the fixed expression rather than a full conjugation table.

## 💡 Remember

> Do not translate these expressions word-for-word. Learn `tener + noun` as a complete Spanish phrase.

# Ser and Estar

## 📌 Grammar Rule

Both **ser** and **estar** mean “to be” in English, but the source distinguishes them by context.

- **ser**: identity, origin, time/date, occupation, relationships, and characteristics presented as defining or inherent.
- **estar**: temporary states and conditions, emotions, health conditions, locations, and situations that can change.

> ==The distinction is about how the speaker presents the information, not an absolute rule that something can never change.==

## 🧩 Structure

### Ser

`subject + ser + information about identity/origin/occupation/etc.`

### Estar

`subject + estar + state/location/situation`

### Location rule

- People and things: **estar**
- Events: **ser**

## 📝 Examples

### Ser

- `Yo soy alto.` — I am tall.
- `Ella es inteligente.` — She is intelligent.
- `Nosotros somos amigos.` — We are friends.
- `El coche es rojo.` — The car is red.
- `María es doctora.` — María is a doctor.
- `Ellos son de México.` — They are from Mexico.
- `Es lunes.` — It is Monday.
- `Yo soy profesora.` — I am a teacher.
- `Ella es mi hermana.` — She is my sister.
- `Juan es doctor.` — Juan is a doctor.
- `Nosotros somos hermanos.` — We are brothers.
- `La comida es deliciosa.` — The food is delicious.
- `El concierto es divertido.` — The concert is fun.
- `Mis amigos son divertidos.` — My friends are fun.
- `Él es simpático.` — He is nice.
- `Tú eres inteligente.` — You are intelligent.
- `Ellos son músicos.` — They are musicians.

### Estar

- `Estoy cansado.` — I am tired.
- `Estás enferma.` — You are sick.
- `Estamos ocupados.` — We are busy.
- `Está feliz.` — He/She is happy.
- `Estás nervioso.` — You are nervous.
- `Estoy preocupado.` — I am worried.
- `Estamos en casa.` — We are at home.
- `El libro está en la mesa.` — The book is on the table.
- `Están en el parque.` — They are at the park.
- `Está abierta.` — It is open.
- `Estoy de vacaciones.` — I am on vacation.
- `Está lloviendo.` — It is raining.
- `Yo estoy en casa.` — I am at home.
- `Ellos están aquí.` — They are here.
- `Tú estás feliz.` — You are happy.
- `Sara está en la escuela.` — Sara is at school.
- `Ellas están contentas.` — They are happy.
- `Estoy emocionado.` — I am excited.
- `Nosotros estamos listos.` — We are ready.
- `Ana está triste.` — Ana is sad.

### Contrast pairs

| Spanish | English | Source explanation |
|---|---|---|
| `Él es doctor.` | He is a doctor. | Occupation/professional identity uses **ser**. |
| `Él está cansado.` | He is tired. | Fatigue is a temporary state. |
| `Nosotros somos de México.` | We are from Mexico. | Origin uses **ser**. |
| `Nosotros estamos en México.` | We are in Mexico. | Location uses **estar**. |
| `Yo soy alta.` | I am tall. | Defining/characteristic presentation. |
| `Yo estoy cansada.` | I am tired. | Current condition. |
| `La mesa está en la cocina.` | The table is in the kitchen. | Correct location of a thing. |
| `La fiesta es en mi casa.` | The party is at my house. | Location of an event uses **ser**. |

### Adjectives that change meaning with ser/estar

| Adjective | With ser | With estar |
|---|---|---|
| `seguro` | `Él es seguro.` — He is confident. | `Él está seguro.` — He is sure. |
| `listo` | `Ella es lista.` — She is clever. | `Ella está lista.` — She is ready. |
| `aburrido` | `El libro es aburrido.` — The book is boring. | `Estoy aburrido.` — I am bored. |

### Idiomatic expressions from the source

With **ser**:

- `El examen fue pan comido.` — The exam was a piece of cake.
- `Ellos son uña y carne.` — They are inseparable.
- `Me siento un cero a la izquierda en esas reuniones.` — I feel like a nobody in those meetings.

With **estar**:

- `Siempre estás en las nubes.` — You are always daydreaming.
- `Él está como una cabra.` — He is a little crazy.
- `Estoy hasta las narices de tus quejas.` — I am fed up with your complaints.

## ⚠️ Important Notes

- Do not use **ser** for the ordinary location of people or things; use **estar**.
- Use **ser** for the location of an event.
- Some adjectives change meaning depending on whether they are used with `ser` or `estar`.
- The source says to learn idiomatic expressions as complete expressions because their meanings may not translate word-for-word.

# Spanish Questions

## 📌 Grammar Rule

The source identifies three useful types of questions in Spanish:

1. **Yes/no questions**: normally invite a `sí` or `no` answer.
2. **Open-ended questions**: request specific information and commonly begin with an interrogative word or phrase.
3. **Tag questions**: add a short tag to seek confirmation or agreement.

Spanish does **not** normally require subject-verb inversion to form a question.

## 🧩 Structure

### Yes/no questions

A statement can become a question through **question marks and intonation** without changing the verb form.

`¿statement word order?`

The subject may remain before the verb or follow the verb:

- `¿Ella come manzanas?`
- `¿Come ella manzanas?`

### Open-ended questions

`interrogative word/phrase + flexible word order`

### Tag questions

`statement + short tag`

Common tags: `¿verdad?`, `¿no?`, `¿cierto?`, `¿no es así?`

## 📝 Examples

### Yes/no questions

- `¿Tienes hambre?` — Are you hungry?
- `¿Vienes con nosotros?` — Are you coming with us?
- `¿Ella come manzanas?` — Does she eat apples?
- `¿Come ella manzanas?` — Does she eat apples?
- `¿Escribes tú cartas?` — Do you write letters?
- `¿Tú vienes?` — Are you coming?
- `¿Tú quieres salir?` — Do you want to go out?
- `¿Estás seguro?` — Are you sure?

### Open-ended questions

| Interrogative | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `¿Qué?` | What? |
| `¿Quién? / ¿Quiénes?` | Who? |
| `¿Dónde?` | Where? |
| `¿Cuándo?` | When? |
| `¿Por qué?` | Why? |
| `¿Cómo?` | How? |
| `¿Cuánto / cuánta / cuántos / cuántas?` | How much? / How many? |

Examples:

- `¿Qué haces?` — What are you doing?
- `¿Cómo te llamas?` — What is your name?
- `¿Dónde vives?` — Where do you live?
- `¿Dónde estás?` — Where are you?
- `¿Por qué estudias español?` — Why are you studying Spanish?
- `¿Cuántos libros tienes?` — How many books do you have?
- `¿Dónde está el baño?` — Where is the bathroom?
- `¿Qué hora es?` — What time is it?
- `¿Cómo estás?` — How are you?
- `¿Cuánto cuesta esto?` — How much does this cost?
- `¿Qué necesitas?` — What do you need?
- `¿Cuándo nos vemos?` — When do we meet?
- `¿Qué quieres comer?` — What do you want to eat?
- `¿Qué opinas?` — What do you think?
- `¿Qué estudias?` — What do you study?
- `¿Cuál es tu color favorito?` — What is your favorite color?
- `¿Cuándo sale el tren?` — When does the train leave?
- `¿Cuándo es la fiesta?` — When is the party?

### Tag questions

- `Estás cansado, ¿verdad?` — You are tired, right?
- `Es tu hermano, ¿no?` — He is your brother, right?
- `Hace mucho calor hoy, ¿verdad?` — It's very hot today, right?
- `Tú eres mi amigo, ¿no?` — You're my friend, right?
- `Estamos listos para irnos, ¿cierto?` — We're ready to leave, right?
- `Ella es la nueva profesora, ¿no es así?` — She is the new teacher, isn't she?
- `Vienes a la fiesta, ¿verdad?` — You're coming to the party, right?
- `No te gusta el café, ¿no?` — You do not like coffee, right?

## ⚠️ Important Notes

- Direct questions use **opening `¿` and closing `?`** in writing.
- In yes/no questions, the subject may stay before the verb or follow it.
- Open-ended question word order is flexible; the source says not to assume one fixed order.
- Interrogative words carry an **accent mark** in direct questions.
- `Cuanto` agrees in gender and number with the noun when it modifies one: `cuánto`, `cuánta`, `cuántos`, `cuántas`.
- Tag questions are generally **invariable**: the tag does not change with the gender or number of the person addressed.
- Intonation can affect emphasis and nuance. The source notes that a yes/no question commonly has rising pitch toward the end, but exact intonation varies by region and context.
- Stress can highlight contrast or express surprise, but the words alone do not determine one fixed interpretation.

## 🔄 Conjugation / Forms

No special question conjugation is required for the question types described in the source.

## 💡 Remember

> Spanish questions usually depend on **question marks, interrogative words, word order, and intonation**, not on a special auxiliary verb or mandatory subject-verb inversion.

# Spanish Prepositions

## 📌 Grammar Rule

A **preposition** shows the relationship between nouns, pronouns, and other words in a sentence. In Spanish, prepositions express relationships of **time, place, direction, manner, and more**.

## 🧩 Structure

Common patterns include:

`preposition + noun/pronoun`

and verb-preposition combinations such as:

`verb + preposition`

Examples: `pensar en`, `soñar con`, `hablar de`, `depender de`, `preocuparse por`, `acordarse de`.

## 📝 Examples

### Common prepositions

| Preposition | Meaning in source | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `a` | to, at | `a la tienda` — I'm going to the store. |
| `ante` | before, in front of | `Hablé ante el público.` — I spoke before the audience. |
| `bajo` | under, below | `El gato está bajo la mesa.` — The cat is under the table. |
| `con` | with | `Voy con mi amigo.` — I'm going with my friend. |
| `contra` | against | `Estoy contra la pared.` — I'm against the wall. |
| `de` | of, from | `El libro de Juan.` — Juan's book. |
| `desde` | from, since | `Trabajo desde las ocho.` — I work from eight o'clock onward. |
| `durante` | during | `Durante la película, no hables.` — Don't talk during the movie. |
| `en` | in, on, at | `Estoy en casa.` — I'm at home. |
| `entre` | between, among | `Entre tú y yo.` — Between you and me. |
| `hacia` | toward | `Camina hacia la puerta.` — Walk toward the door. |
| `hasta` | until, up to | `Trabajaré hasta las cinco.` — I'll work until five. |
| `para` | for, in order to | `Este regalo es para ti.` — This gift is for you. |
| `por` | for, by, through | `Gracias por tu ayuda.` — Thanks for your help. |
| `según` | according to | `Según el profesor, el examen será fácil.` — According to the teacher, the exam will be easy. |
| `sin` | without | `Café sin azúcar.` — Coffee without sugar. |
| `sobre` | on, about | `El libro está sobre la mesa.` — The book is on the table. |
| `tras` | after, behind | `Tras la puerta hay un armario.` — Behind the door there is a wardrobe. |

### Prepositions of place

- `en` — on, at: `La pluma está en la mesa.` — The pen is on the table.
- `sobre` — on top of: `El libro está sobre el escritorio.` — The book is on the desk.
- `debajo de` — under: `El gato está debajo de la cama.` — The cat is under the bed.
- `encima de` — on top of: `Hay una lámpara encima de la mesa.` — There is a lamp on top of the table.
- `detrás de` — behind: `El jardín está detrás de la casa.` — The garden is behind the house.
- `delante de` — in front of: `Hay un árbol delante de mi ventana.` — There is a tree in front of my window.
- `entre` — between: `El banco está entre la tienda y el restaurante.` — The bench is between the store and the restaurant.
- `cerca de` — near: `Vivo cerca del parque.` — I live near the park.
- `lejos de` — far from: `La playa está lejos de aquí.` — The beach is far from here.

### Prepositions of time and manner

**Time**

- `a` — at, for specific times: `La reunión es a las tres.` — The meeting is at three.
- `de` — from, during a period: `Trabajamos de nueve a cinco.` — We work from nine to five.
- `desde` — since, from a starting point: `Estoy esperando desde las dos.` — I have been waiting since two.
- `hasta` — until: `Estaré aquí hasta las seis.` — I'll be here until six.

# Possessive Pronouns

## 📌 Grammar Rule

Spanish **possessive pronouns** replace a noun phrase and indicate ownership or association.

They agree in **gender and number with the noun they replace**, not with the possessor.

> ==The noun being replaced determines the possessive pronoun's gender and number.==

The source distinguishes **unstressed possessive adjectives** (`mi`, `tu`, `su`, etc.) from **stressed possessive forms** (`mío`, `tuyo`, `suyo`, etc.).

## 🧩 Structure

### Possessive adjective before a noun

`possessive adjective + noun`

Examples: `mi casa`, `tus libros`, `nuestra mesa`

### Stressed possessive pronoun replacing a noun phrase

`definite article + stressed possessive`

Examples: `el mío`, `la tuya`, `los nuestros`, `las suyas`

### After ser

The stressed possessive normally appears **without an article**:

`noun + ser + stressed possessive`

Example: `El libro es mío.`

## 📝 Examples

### Stressed possessive forms

| Possessor | Masculine singular | Feminine singular | Masculine plural | Feminine plural |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| mine | `mío` | `mía` | `míos` | `mías` |
| yours, informal singular | `tuyo` | `tuya` | `tuyos` | `tuyas` |
| his/hers/yours formal singular/theirs | `suyo` | `suya` | `suyos` | `suyas` |
| ours | `nuestro` | `nuestra` | `nuestros` | `nuestras` |
| yours, informal plural in Spain | `vuestro` | `vuestra` | `vuestros` | `vuestras` |

### Unstressed possessive adjectives

| Number / form | Forms | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Singular | `mi`, `tu`, `su` | `mi perro`, `tu libro`, `su coche` |
| Plural | `mis`, `tus`, `sus` | `mis perros`, `tus libros`, `sus coches` |
| Masculine with nuestro/vuestro | `nuestro`, `vuestro` | `nuestro amigo`, `vuestro libro` |
| Feminine with nuestro/vuestro | `nuestra`, `vuestra` | `nuestra amiga`, `vuestra mesa` |

### Gender and number agreement

- `El libro es mío.` — The book is mine.
- `Los libros son míos.` — The books are mine.
- `La casa es tuya.` — The house is yours.
- `Las casas son tuyas.` — The houses are yours.
- `El coche es nuestro.` — The car is ours.
- `La casa es nuestra.` — The house is ours.
- `El libro es vuestro.` — The book is yours (informal plural, in Spain).
- `Las mesas son vuestras.` — The tables are yours (informal plural, in Spain).

### Stressed vs. unstressed forms

Unstressed forms:

- `mi casa` — my house
- `tus libros` — your books
- `nuestras sillas` — our chairs

Stressed forms:

- `El libro es mío.` — The book is mine.
- `La casa tuya es más grande.` — Your house is bigger.
- `Los libros suyos están aquí.` — His, hers, yours, or their books are here.
- `Esa es la mochila tuya, no la mía.` — That is your backpack, not mine.

### Formal and informal examples

- `¿Es su casa, señor?` — Is it your house, sir?
- `¿La casa es suya?` — Is the house yours?
- `¿Es tu casa?` — Is it your house?
- `¿La casa es tuya?` — Is the house yours?

## ⚠️ Important Notes

- **Unstressed** forms come before the noun: `mi`, `tu`, `su`, `nuestro`, `vuestro`, etc.
- **Stressed** forms can stand alone, commonly with a definite article, or can follow a noun for emphasis or contrast.
- When a stressed possessive replaces a noun phrase, the definite article agrees with the omitted noun: `el mío`, `la tuya`, `los nuestros`, `las suyas`.
- Do **not** use an article before an unstressed possessive adjective directly before a noun in standard Spanish: `mi libro`, not `el mi libro`.
- After **ser**, the stressed possessive normally appears without an article: `El libro es mío.`
- `suyo` can be ambiguous: it may mean his, hers, yours (formal), or theirs. Context or an added phrase such as `de él` or `de ellos` may be needed.
- For plural `ustedes`, regional usage matters. In most of Latin America, `ustedes` is used for both formal and informal plural address, so `su` and `suyo` serve both contexts. In Spain, `vosotros` normally takes `vuestro`, while formal plural `ustedes` takes `su`.

### Common mistakes from the source

- Incorrect agreement: `El mío` cannot replace `la casa`; the correct form is `la mía`.
- Wrong article construction: `Quiero el mío.` but `El libro es mío.`
- Confusing adjective and pronoun: `Es mi libro. Es mío.`
- Wrong gender/number: `Las casas son mías`, not `Las casas son mío.`
- Ambiguous `suyo`: `El coche es suyo, el de Ana.`

## 🔄 Conjugation / Forms

### Pronoun forms

| Person / meaning | Unstressed adjective | Stressed possessive |
|---|---|---|
| mine | `mi / mis` | `mío, mía, míos, mías` |
| yours, informal singular | `tu / tus` | `tuyo, tuya, tuyos, tuyas` |
| his/hers/yours formal/theirs | `su / sus` | `suyo, suya, suyos, suyas` |

| yours, informal plural Spain | `vuestro/a/os/as` | `vuestro, vuestra, vuestros, vuestras` |

## 💡 Remember

> **Possessive adjectives** accompany a noun: `mi libro`. **Possessive pronouns** replace the noun: `el mío` / `El libro es mío`.

> The possessive pronoun agrees with the **thing possessed**, not the person who possesses it.

# Spanish Negation

## 📌 Grammar Rule

Spanish uses negative words to deny or limit an action, event, person, thing, or possibility.

The most common negative words shown in the source are:

- `no` — not
- `nunca` — never
- `jamás` — never / ever
- `nadie` — nobody / no one
- `nada` — nothing / anything in a negative sentence
- `ningún` — no / not any before a masculine singular noun
- `ni` — nor / not even in certain expressions
- `tampoco` — neither / not either

## 🧩 Structure

### Basic negative sentence

`subject + no + conjugated verb + rest of sentence`

Example: `Yo no entiendo.`

The subject is often omitted because the verb ending identifies it:

`no + conjugated verb`

### Negative word after the verb

`no + verb + negative word`

Examples:

- `No veo nada.`
- `No hay nadie aquí.`
- `No tengo ningún problema.`

### Negative word before the verb

A negative word before the verb normally makes an additional `no` unnecessary:

`negative word + verb`

Examples:

- `Nadie vino.`
- `Nunca voy allí.`
- `Ni siquiera llamó.`

### Negative commands and pronouns

- Affirmative command: pronoun attaches to the verb: `Háblame.`
- Negative command: pronoun comes before the verb: `No me hables.`

## 📝 Examples

### Negative words

- `No come carne.` — He/She does not eat meat.
- `Nunca he estado allí.` — I have never been there.
- `Nadie sabe la respuesta.` — No one knows the answer.
- `No hay nada aquí.` — There is nothing here.
- `Juan no trabaja los fines de semana tampoco.` — Juan does not work on weekends either.

### Negation and verb tense

- Present: `Yo no hablo.` — I do not speak.
- Past: `Él no comió.` — He did not eat.
- Future: `Nosotros no iremos allí.` — We will not live there.
- Compound: `Yo no he terminado.` — I have not finished.
- Verb phrase: `Ella no puede venir.` — She cannot come.

### Negation and pronouns

- `No lo tengo.` — I do not have it.
- `No le hablo.` — I do not talk to him or her.

## ⚠️ Important Notes

- Spanish uses **negative concord**: multiple negative words can work together to express one negative meaning.
- A postverbal `nadie`, `nada`, or `ningún` normally appears with `no`.
- A negative word placed before the verb normally replaces `no`.
- `nadie` refers to people; `nada` refers to things or actions.
- Use `ningún` before a masculine singular noun; related forms include `ninguna`, `ninguno`, and `ningunos`.
- Negation does **not** change the verb's tense, person, or conjugation ending.

# Spanish Adjectives

## 📌 Grammar Rule

`noun + adjective`

### Descriptive vs limiting modifiers

Descriptive adjectives characterize a noun:

- `Es un libro interesante.` — It is an interesting book.

- `Oigo perros ruidosos.` — I hear noisy dogs.

Limiting modifiers specify or restrict a noun. The source includes articles, demonstratives, possessives, interrogatives, and quantifiers:

- `Compré el libro.` — I bought the book.

### Adjective clauses / relative clauses

An adjective clause is a subordinate clause that describes or gives more information about a noun.

Common relative words/forms shown in the source:

- `que` — that, which, who
- `quien`, `quienes` — who, whom (normally people)
- `cuyo`, `cuya`, `cuyos`, `cuyas` — whose
- `el cual`, `la cual`, `los cuales`, `las cuales` — which, who
- `el que`, `la que`, `los que`, `las que` — that, which, who
- `lo que`, `lo cual` — what, which, referring to an idea or unspecified thing

Defining clause:

- `El libro que compré ayer es muy interesante.` — The book that I bought yesterday is very interesting.

Non-defining clause:

- `Mi hermano, quien es médico, vive en Madrid.` — My brother, who is a doctor, lives in Madrid.

# Personal Pronouns

## 📌 Grammar Rule

Spanish personal pronouns vary according to their **grammatical function, person, number, and sometimes gender**.

The source distinguishes:

- **Subject pronouns**: subjects of verbs.
- **Object pronouns**: direct or indirect objects.
- **Possessive forms**: indicate ownership.
- **Reflexive pronouns**: show that the subject performs the action on themself or themselves.

Because Spanish verb endings often identify the subject, subject pronouns are frequently omitted when the meaning is clear.

## 🧩 Structure

### Subject pronouns

`subject pronoun + conjugated verb`

But the pronoun can be omitted when clear:

`conjugated verb`

### Object pronouns

They normally come before a conjugated verb:

`object pronoun + conjugated verb`

They can attach to an infinitive:

`infinitive + object pronoun`

When both indirect and direct object pronouns occur together:

`indirect object pronoun + direct object pronoun + verb`

and **le/les -> se** before `lo`, `la`, `los`, or `las`.

## 📝 Examples

### Subject pronouns

| Person | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| First | `yo` | `nosotros / nosotras` |
| Second, informal | `tú` | `vosotros / vosotras` (mainly Spain) |
| Second, formal | `usted` | `ustedes` |
| Third | `él / ella` | `ellos / ellas` |

Examples:

- `Yo soy estudiante.` — I am a student.
- `Tú eres mi amigo.` — You are my friend.
- `Usted es el profesor.` — You are the teacher.
- `Él es médico.` — He is a doctor.
- `Nosotros somos vecinos.` — We are neighbors.
- `Vosotros vais al parque.` — You all are going to the park.
- `Ustedes trabajan aquí.` — You all work here.
- `Ellos estudian español.` — They study Spanish.

### Object pronouns

| Form | Function / source gloss |
|---|---|
| `me` | me |
| `te` | you, singular informal |
| `lo / la` | him, it, or her as a direct object |
| `le` | to him, to her, or to you as an indirect object |
| `nos` | us |
| `os` | you all, informal, mainly Spain |
| `los / las` | them as a direct object |
| `les` | to them or to you all as an indirect object |

Examples:

- `La veo.` — I see her.
- `Voy a verla.` — I am going to see her.
- `Le doy un regalo.` — I give him, her, or you a gift.
- `Voy a darle un regalo.` — I am going to give him, her, or you a gift.
- `Se lo doy.` — I give it to him, her, or you.
- `Vi a Juan y le di el libro.` — I saw John and gave him the book.

### Pronoun attachment

Object and reflexive pronouns can attach to:

- affirmative commands
- gerunds
- infinitives

An accent mark may be required to preserve the original stress when a pronoun attaches to a gerund or command.

Examples:

- `Dámelo.` — Give it to me.
- `Estoy haciéndolo.` — I am doing it.
- `Necesito comprarlo.` — I need to buy it.

### Reflexive pronouns

- `Me lavo.` — I wash myself.
- `Voy a lavarme.` — I am going to wash myself.
- `Estoy lavándome.` — I am washing myself.

## ⚠️ Important Notes

- Subject pronouns are often omitted when the verb ending makes the subject clear.
- A speaker may include a subject pronoun for contrast, emphasis, or extra clarity.
- Object and possessive forms have different functions and should not be confused with subject pronouns.
- When direct and indirect object pronouns appear together, the indirect object pronoun comes first.
- `le` or `les` changes to `se` before `lo`, `la`, `los`, or `las`.
- Pronouns are positioned differently with affirmative and negative commands.
- `Vosotros/vosotras` is used mainly in Spain; most of Latin America uses `ustedes` for plural “you.”

# Spanish Noun Plurals

## 📌 Grammar Rule

Spanish noun plurals are usually formed according to the noun's final letter or sound. Articles, adjectives, and other words also show plural agreement.

## 🧩 Structure

### Basic patterns

- `unstressed vowel + s`
- `consonant + es`
- `z -> c + es`

Examples:

- `casa -> casas`
- `animal -> animales`
- `luz -> luces`

## 📝 Examples

### Nouns ending in vowels

- `La casa es grande. Las casas son grandes.` — The house is big. The houses are big.
- `El perro corre. Los perros corren.` — The dog runs. The dogs run.
- `El rubí brilla. Los rubíes brillan.` — The ruby shines. The rubies shine.
- `El tabú es antiguo. Los tabúes son antiguos.` — The taboo is old. The taboos are old.
- `El café está caliente. Los cafés están calientes.` — The coffee is hot. The coffees are hot.

### Nouns ending in consonants

- `El animal duerme. Los animales duermen.` — The animal is sleeping. The animals are sleeping.
- `El reloj funciona. Los relojes funcionan.` — The watch works. The watches work.
- `La mujer sonríe. Las mujeres sonríen.` — The woman smiles. The women smile.

### Nouns ending in -z

Replace `z` with `c` and add `-es`:

| Singular | Plural |
|---|---|
| `pez` | `peces` |
| `voz` | `voces` |
| `luz` | `luces` |
| `lápiz` | `lápices` |

Examples:

- `El pez nada. Los peces nadan.` — The fish swims. The fish swim.
- `La voz es clara. Las voces son claras.` — The voice is clear. The voices are clear.
- `La luz está encendida. Las luces están encendidas.` — The light is on. The lights are on.
- `El lápiz está en la mesa. Los lápices están en la mesa.` — The pencil is on the table. The pencils are on the table.

### Nouns ending in unstressed -s or -x

These generally do **not** change in the plural; the article and other words indicate the number.

- `El lunes es festivo. Los lunes son festivos.` — Monday is a holiday. Mondays are holidays.
- `El tórax está sano. Los tórax están sanos.` — The thorax is healthy. The thoraxes are healthy.

### Stressed final vowels / -s

The source notes that nouns ending in a stressed vowel commonly add `-es` in some patterns, while nouns ending in stressed `-á`, `-é`, or `-ó` normally add `-s` in its examples.

- `Dos gatos y dos gatas son cuatro gatos.` — Two male cats and two female cats are four cats.

# Gender of Spanish Nouns

## 📌 Grammar Rule

Spanish nouns are grammatically **masculine or feminine**. Gender affects articles and adjectives.

The source gives common ending patterns, but emphasizes that they are **general patterns, not absolute rules**.

> ==Learning a noun together with its article is useful.==

## 🧩 Structure

### Common patterns

- Masculine nouns often end in `-o`.
- Feminine nouns often end in `-a`.
- Other endings such as `-e` or consonants can be masculine or feminine.
- Gender determines article choice and affects adjective agreement.

## 📝 Examples

### Basic gender patterns

- `el libro` — the book
- `el perro` — the dog
- `la mesa` — the table
- `la casa` — the house
- `el día` — the day
- `la mano` — the hand
- `el coche` — the car
- `la noche` — the night

### Masculine endings

The source lists these common patterns:

- `-o`: `el libro`, `el perro`
- `-ma`: many Greek-origin nouns: `el problema`, `el tema`
- `-or`: `el amor`, `el calor`
- `-aje`: `el garaje`, `el paisaje`
- `-án` and `-ambre`: `el refrán`, `el hambre`

Other masculine examples:

- `el día`
- `el mapa`
- `el clima`
- `el planeta`
- `el sistema`

Special point from the source:

- `el águila` is **feminine** despite taking `el` in the singular before its stressed initial `a`.

### Feminine endings

- `-a`: `la casa`, `la manzana`
- `-ción` / `-sión`: `la nación`, `la televisión`
- `-dad`: `la ciudad`, `la universidad`

Exceptions include:

- `el día`
- `el mapa`
- Greek-origin masculine nouns such as `el tema` and `el problema`
- `la mano`
- `la radio`

Some nouns can change gender when their meaning changes.

| Masculine | Meaning | Feminine | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `el capital` | money / financial assets | `la capital` | capital city |
| `el guía` | male guide | `la guía` | female guide / guidebook |
| `el orden` | order, arrangement, tidiness | `la orden` | command / religious order |

### Invariable nouns for people

Some nouns have the same form for male and female people; the article and adjectives show the gender:

- `el cantante / la cantante` — the male/female singer
- `el artista / la artista` — the male/female artist
- `el estudiante / la estudiante` — the male/female student

Examples:

- `El cantante es muy talentoso.` — The male singer is very talented.
- `La cantante es muy talentosa.` — The female singer is very talented.

### Compound noun gender

The source says the gender of compounds is often determined by the main word or by established usage, so it is best to learn each compound with its article.

Examples:

- `el abrelatas` — the can opener
- `el sacapuntas` — the pencil sharpener
- `el paraguas` — the umbrella
- `el lavaplatos` — the dishwasher
- `el cumpleaños` — the birthday
- `el rompecabezas` — the puzzle

`el salvavidas / la salvavidas` can refer to a male/female lifesaver; the object is usually masculine.

### Gender affects articles and adjectives

- Masculine singular: `El libro rojo.` — The red book.
- Feminine singular: `La casa blanca.` — The white house.
- Masculine plural: `Los libros rojos.` — The red books.
- Feminine plural: `Las casas blancas.` — The white houses.
- `Un coche rápido.` — A fast car.
- `Una flor bonita.` — A pretty flower.
- `Unos coches rápidos.` — Some fast cars.
- `Unas flores bonitas.` — Some pretty flowers.

## ⚠️ Important Notes

- Ending patterns are helpful but **not universal**.
- Some nouns simply have to be learned individually.
- Learn nouns with their articles when a gender pattern is unreliable.
- Article and adjective agreement reflects the noun's gender and number.

- `Necesito cuatro manzanas para el postre.` — I need four apples for the dessert.
- `Compré cinco libros nuevos.` — I bought five new books.

- `La tienda abre a las nueve.` — The store opens at nine.
- `Llevo quince minutos esperando.` — I have been waiting for fifteen minutes.

### Ordinal numbers

Ordinal numbers show position or order. They normally come before the noun and agree in gender and number.

| Number | Ordinal |
|---:|---|
| 1 | `primero/a` |
| 2 | `segundo/a` |
| 3 | `tercero/a` |
| 4 | `cuarto/a` |
| 5 | `quinto/a` |
| 6 | `sexto/a` |
| 7 | `séptimo/a` |
| 8 | `octavo/a` |
| 9 | `noveno/a` |
| 10 | `décimo/a` |

Before a masculine singular noun:

- `primero -> primer`
- `tercero -> tercer`

Examples:

- `El primer capítulo.` — The first chapter.
- `El tercer día.` — The third day.
- `La primera vez.` — The first time.
- `Quedó en segundo lugar.` — He/She came in second place.
- `Vivo en la tercera casa.` — I live in the third house.
- `Es el cuarto día.` — It is the fourth day.
- `La quinta avenida está cerca.` — Fifth Avenue is nearby.
- `El sexto sentido.` — The sixth sense.
- `La séptima maravilla.` — The seventh wonder.
- `Vivo en el octavo piso.` — I live on the eighth floor.
- `Está en noveno grado.` — He/She is in ninth grade.
- `Es la décima vez.` — It is the tenth time.

### Multiplicative numbers

Multiplicative numbers express how many times an amount is multiplied. They can function as adjectives or nouns and agree in gender and number when they modify a noun.

| Number | Spanish |
|---:|---|

| 3 | `triple` |
| 4 | `cuádruple` |

Examples:

- `El resultado es el doble.` — The result is double.

### Collective numbers

Collective terms name a group or quantity as a single unit. The source treats them as nouns, so they normally occur with an article or another determiner.

| Spanish | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `docena` | a dozen / 12 units |
| `decena` | a group of ten |
| `centena` | a hundred |
| `millar` | a thousand |
| `quincena` | a period of 15 days / a fortnight |

Examples:

- `Compré una docena de huevos.` — I bought a dozen eggs.
- `La biblioteca tiene más de una centena de libros raros.` — The library has more than a hundred rare books.

- `El equipo ha ganado una decena de premios este año.` — The team has won ten awards this year.
- `Tendré vacaciones durante la quincena del festival.` — I will have a two-week vacation during the festival period.

### Fractions

Common fractions:

- `un medio` — one half (1/2)

- `un tercio`

- `uno` changes to `una` before a feminine noun in the source's examples: `veintiuna personas`, `treinta y una páginas`.

- In Spanish monetary notation, decimal comma usage and the placement of currency symbols vary by country/style.

| Context | Form |
|---|---|
| exact 100 | `cien` |
| 101+ when another number follows | `ciento...` |

# Articles in Spanish

## 📌 Grammar Rule

Spanish has **four definite articles** and **four indefinite articles**, and they agree with the noun in gender and number.

### Definite articles

- `el` — masculine singular
- `la` — feminine singular
- `los` — masculine plural
- `las` — feminine plural

### Indefinite articles

- `un` — masculine singular
- `una` — feminine singular
- `unos` — masculine plural
- `unas` — feminine plural

## 🧩 Structure

### Definite article

`article + noun`

Examples: `el libro`, `la casa`, `los libros`, `las casas`

### Indefinite article

`article + noun`

Examples: `un libro`, `una casa`, `unos libros`, `unas casas`

## 📝 Examples

### Definite articles

- `El libro está aquí.` — The book is here.
- `El perro duerme.` — The dog is sleeping.
- `La casa es grande.` — The house is big.
- `La mesa está limpia.` — The table is clean.
- `Los libros están aquí.` — The books are here.
- `Los perros duermen.` — The dogs are sleeping.
- `Las casas son grandes.` — The houses are big.
- `Las mesas están limpias.` — The tables are clean.
- `El libro que me prestaste es interesante.` — The book you lent me is interesting.
- `La luna está llena.` — The moon is full.
- `La educación es importante.` — Education is important.
- `El lunes empieza el curso.` — The course starts on Monday.
- `El señor Pérez llegó temprano.` — Mr. Pérez arrived early.

### Indefinite articles

- `Un libro está sobre la mesa.` — A book is on the table.
- `Una casa está en venta.` — A house is for sale.
- `Unos amigos vienen esta noche.` — Some friends are coming tonight.
- `Unas manzanas están maduras.` — Some apples are ripe.

## ⚠️ Important Notes

### When to use definite articles

The source lists these uses:

1. Specific or previously mentioned things: `El libro que me prestaste es interesante.`
2. Unique things such as the moon and sun: `La luna está llena.`
3. General categories or abstract concepts: `La educación es importante.`
4. Before days of the week and, commonly, seasons: `El lunes empieza el curso.`
5. Before a title when it is used as a noun phrase rather than direct address: `El señor Pérez llegó temprano.`

### Exceptions / special uses

- **Titles in direct address**: the article is normally omitted.
  - `Señora García, ¿puede ayudarme?` — Mrs. García, can you help me?
  - With the title + name as an ordinary noun phrase, the article is generally used: `La señora García llegó.` — Mrs. García arrived.

- **Days of the week**: Spanish normally uses a definite article.
  - `El lunes tengo una cita.` — I have an appointment on Monday.
  - `Trabajo los viernes.` — I work on Fridays.

- **Abstract nouns**: definite articles are frequently used.
  - `La libertad es importante.` — Freedom is important.
  - `El amor puede cambiar a una persona.` — Love can change a person.

- **Languages**: after `hablar` and `aprender`, the language name is typically used without an article, but an article can be used when the language is treated as a specific subject/object.
  - `Hablo español.` — I speak Spanish.
  - `Estoy aprendiendo el español de México.` — I am learning the Spanish of Mexico.

- **Professions after ser**: a profession is usually used without an indefinite article when it simply identifies someone's occupation. The article is used when the profession is qualified or presented as a specific individual.
  - `Es ingeniero.` — He is an engineer.
  - `Es un ingeniero muy reconocido.` — He is a highly regarded engineer.

- **Preposition + article combinations** shown in the source:
  - `Voy a la ciudad.` — I am going to the city.
  - `Vengo del mercado.` — I am coming from the market.

- **Idiomatic expression**:
  - `¿Me haces el favor?` — Could you do me the favor?

### Mixed-gender groups

For mixed-gender groups, the source says Spanish traditionally uses the masculine plural form `los`.

Example:

`Los niños y las niñas...`

## 🔄 Conjugation / Forms

| Number | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| Singular definite | `el` | `la` |
| Plural definite | `los` | `las` |
| Singular indefinite | `un` | `una` |
| Plural indefinite | `unos` | `unas` |

## 💡 Remember

> ==Articles must agree with the noun in gender and number.==

> Pay special attention to **days of the week, titles in direct address, abstract nouns, language names, professions, and article combinations**.

# Spanish Present Tense

## 📌 Grammar Rule

The **presente de indicativo** is used for:

- actions happening now
- habitual actions
- general truths
- some near or scheduled future events
- storytelling/narration (historical present)

Spanish often omits the subject pronoun because the **verb ending identifies the subject**.

## 🧩 Structure

### Regular present tense

`infinitive - infinitive ending + present-tense ending`

Regular verbs follow separate patterns for `-ar`, `-er`, and `-ir` verbs.

### Reflexive verbs

`reflexive pronoun + conjugated verb`

Example: `Me levanto.`

### Stem-changing verbs

The source gives three main patterns:

- `e -> ie`
- `o -> ue`
- `e -> i`

The change usually occurs in all forms except **nosotros** and **vosotros**.

## 📝 Examples

### Uses of the present tense

- General truth: `El sol sale por el este.` — The sun rises in the east.
- Habitual action: `Estudio todas las noches.` — I study every night.
- Action happening now: `Ella come una manzana.` — She is eating an apple.
- Future action: `Mañana viajo a Madrid.` — I am traveling to Madrid tomorrow.
- Storytelling/narration: `El héroe llega al castillo y rescata a la princesa.` — The hero arrives at the castle and rescues the princess.

Common time expressions shown:

- `ahora` — now
- `hoy` — today
- `cada día` — every day
- `normalmente` — normally
- `mañana` — tomorrow

### Present-tense examples

- `Yo tengo un perro.` — I have a dog.
- `Ella bebe agua.` — She drinks water.
- `Tú comes pizza.` — You eat pizza.
- `Tú compras pan.` — You buy bread.
- `Nosotros estudiamos español.` — We study Spanish.
- `Yo miro las estrellas.` — I look at the stars.
- `Ella vende flores.` — She sells flowers.
- `Tú cocinas pasta.` — You cook pasta.
- `Él toca la guitarra.` — He plays the guitar.
- `Tú escuchas música.` — You listen to music.
- `Ellos ven una película.` — They watch a movie.
- `Ellos escuchan música.` — They listen to music.
- `Nosotros aprendemos juntos.` — We learn together.
- `Ella enseña inglés.` — She teaches English.
- `Yo nado en el mar.` — I swim in the sea.
- `Ellos juegan al fútbol.` — They play soccer.
- `Yo bebo café.` — I drink coffee.
- `Yo como una manzana.` — I eat an apple.
- `Tú abres la puerta.` — You open the door.
- `Ella dibuja muy bien.` — She draws very well.

## ⚠️ Important Notes

### Regular endings

### Stem-changing verbs

The vowel change generally does not occur in the first- and second-person plural forms (`nosotros`, `vosotros`) in the source's examples.
