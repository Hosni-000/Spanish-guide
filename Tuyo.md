# 🇪🇸 Tuyo — Spanish Study Note

> **Theme:** Possessive love, metaphors, desire  
> **Level:** A1 → A2  
> **Song:** _Tuyo_ — Rodrigo Amarante  
> **From:** _Narcos_

---

## 📝 Lyrics + Translation

### 1. Soy el fuego que arde tu piel

**Soy** = I am  
**el fuego** = the fire  
**que** = that/which  
**arde** = burns  
**tu piel** = your skin

> **Translation:** _I am the fire that burns your skin._

### Grammar

**soy** comes from **ser** = to be.

|Person|Ser|
|---|---|
|yo|**soy**|
|tú|eres|
|él/ella|es|
|nosotros|somos|
|ellos|son|

**arde** comes from **arder** = to burn.

> El fuego **arde**.  
> The fire burns.

Notice:

> **tu** piel = your skin

There is **no accent** on _tu_ here because **tu** = your.

---

### 2. Soy el agua que mata tu sed

**el agua** = the water  
**mata** = kills  
**tu sed** = your thirst

> **Translation:** _I am the water that kills your thirst._

### Grammar

**mata** comes from **matar** = to kill.

> Yo mato  
> Tú matas  
> Él/ella mata

Literally:

> **matar la sed** = to kill thirst

But naturally in English:

> **quench your thirst**

So the poetic meaning is:

> _I am the water that quenches your thirst._

---

### 3. El castillo, la torre yo soy

**el castillo** = the castle  
**la torre** = the tower  
**yo soy** = I am

> **Translation:** _The castle, the tower — I am._

This word order is deliberately poetic.

Normal Spanish would be:

> **Yo soy el castillo, la torre.**

But:

> **El castillo, la torre yo soy**

puts the objects first for dramatic emphasis.

---

### 4. La espada que guarda el caudal

**la espada** = the sword  
**que** = that/which  
**guarda** = guards/keeps  
**el caudal** = the flow / wealth / treasure

> **Translation:** _The sword that guards the treasure._

### Grammar

**guarda** → **guardar** = to guard / keep

> La espada **guarda** el caudal.  
> The sword guards the treasure.

**que** introduces a relative clause:

> la espada **que guarda el caudal**

Literally:

> the sword **that guards the treasure**

---

# 🌙 The Second Half

### 5. Tú, el aire que respiro yo

**tú** = you  
**el aire** = the air  
**que** = that/which  
**respiro** = I breathe  
**yo** = I

> **Translation:** _You, the air that I breathe._

The normal order would be:

> **Tú eres el aire que yo respiro.**

But the song removes **eres** and rearranges things poetically.

### Important grammar

**respiro** comes from **respirar** = to breathe.

Because the ending **-o** tells us the subject is **yo**, Spanish doesn't need to say _yo_.

> **Respiro** = I breathe.

So:

> **el aire que respiro**

= the air that I breathe

---

### 6. Y la luz de la luna en el mar

**y** = and  
**la luz** = the light  
**de la luna** = of the moon / moon's  
**en el mar** = in the sea

> **Translation:** _And the light of the moon on/in the sea._

### Grammar

**de** is extremely important.

> la luz **de** la luna  
> the light **of** the moon

Spanish commonly uses:

> **noun + de + noun**

where English often uses a possessive:

> la luz de la luna  
> the moon's light

---

### 7. La garganta que ansío mojar

**la garganta** = the throat  
**que** = that/which  
**ansío** = I long for / I yearn to  
**mojar** = to wet

> **Translation:** _The throat that I long to wet._

### Grammar

**ansío** comes from **ansiar** = to yearn for / long for.

The important structure is:

> **ansiar + infinitive**

> Ansío **mojar**.  
> I long **to wet**.

Notice that **mojar** stays in the infinitive.

---

### 8. Que temo ahogar de amor

This line is poetic and can feel weird if translated word-for-word.

**que** = that/which  
**temo** = I fear  
**ahogar** = to drown  
**de amor** = with/in love

> **Translation:** _That I fear drowning with love._

Or more poetically:

> _That I fear I might drown in love._

### Grammar

**temo** comes from **temer** = to fear.

> temo + infinitive

> Temo **caer**.  
> I fear falling.

> Temo **perderte**.  
> I fear losing you.

So:

> temo **ahogar**

= I fear drowning.

---

# ❤️ Desire

### 9. ¿Y cuáles deseos me vas a dar, oh?

**y** = and  
**cuáles** = which / what  
**deseos** = desires  
**me** = to me  
**vas a dar** = you are going to give

> **Translation:** _And what desires are you going to give me?_

### 🔥 Important grammar: ir + a + infinitive

This is one of the most useful Spanish future constructions.

> **ir + a + infinitive**

Literally:

> vas + a + dar

= you go + to + give

But naturally:

> **vas a dar** = you are going to give

Examples:

> Voy a comer.  
> I'm going to eat.

> Voy a estudiar.  
> I'm going to study.

> Vas a dormir.  
> You're going to sleep.

> ¿Qué vas a hacer?  
> What are you going to do?

So:

> **¿Qué me vas a dar?**

= What are you going to give me?

---

# 💎 10. Dices tú, mi tesoro, basta con mirarlo

This part is particularly poetic.

**dices** = you say  
**tú** = you  
**mi tesoro** = my treasure  
**basta** = it is enough / suffice  
**con mirarlo** = just by looking at it

A natural interpretation is approximately:

> _You say, my treasure, that it's enough just to look at it._

### Grammar: basta con...

This is a useful construction:

> **basta con + infinitive**

= it's enough to...

Examples:

> **Basta con mirar.**  
> It's enough to look.

> **Basta con preguntar.**  
> It's enough to ask.

> **Basta con intentarlo.**  
> It's enough to try.

---

## 👀 Why "mirarlo"?

This is:

> **mirar + lo**

**mirar** = to look at  
**lo** = it/him

When an object pronoun is attached to an infinitive, it gets written together:

> mirar + lo → **mirarlo**

Examples:

> comer + lo → comerlo  
> to eat it

> ver + lo → verlo  
> to see it

> comprar + lo → comprarlo  
> to buy it

So:

> **basta con mirarlo**

literally:

> _it's enough by looking at it_

---

# 💋 11. Y tuyo será, y tuyo será

**y** = and  
**tuyo** = yours  
**será** = will be

> **Translation:** _And it will be yours, and it will be yours._

### Grammar: possessive pronouns

Spanish has a distinction between:

> **tu** = your  
> **tuyo** = yours

Compare:

> **tu tesoro**  
> your treasure

vs.

> **El tesoro es tuyo.**  
> The treasure is yours.

### Why "tuyo"?

Because **tuyo** replaces the noun.

> Es **tu tesoro**.  
> It is your treasure.

> Es **tuyo**.  
> It is yours.

---

# ⏳ Why "será"?

**será** comes from:

> **ser** = to be

Future tense:

|Person|Ser|
|---|---|
|yo|seré|
|tú|serás|
|él/ella|**será**|
|nosotros|seremos|
|vosotros|seréis|
|ellos|serán|

So:

> **será** = it/he/she will be

In:

> **tuyo será**

the word order is poetic.

Normal:

> **Será tuyo.**  
> It will be yours.

Song:

> **Tuyo será.**

Same basic meaning, but putting **tuyo** first gives it emphasis:

> **YOURS** it will be.

# 🧠 Grammar Cheat Sheet

## 1. Spanish subject pronouns can disappear

Spanish verbs often tell you who is doing the action.

> **Respiro.**  
> I breathe.

You don't need:

> **Yo respiro.**

Both are grammatically correct.

---

## 2. Relative clauses with "que"

**que** often means:

> that / which / who

Examples:

> el fuego **que arde**  
> the fire **that burns**

> el aire **que respiro**  
> the air **that I breathe**

> la espada **que guarda**  
> the sword **that guards**

---

## 3. Possessive adjective vs possessive pronoun

### Possessive adjective

> **mi** = my  
> **tu** = your  
> **su** = his/her/their

They go before a noun:

> mi tesoro  
> my treasure

> tu piel  
> your skin

### Possessive pronoun

> **mío** = mine  
> **tuyo** = yours  
> **suyo** = his/hers/theirs

They stand in for the noun:

> Es **mío**.  
> It's mine.

> Es **tuyo**.  
> It's yours.

# ⚠️ Your Spanish Corrections

You wrote:

> **Dices tu**

The correct written form is:

> **Dices tú**

because **tú** = you.

Meanwhile:

> **tu** = your

Compare:

> **Tú** eres mi amigo.  
> You are my friend.

> **Tu** amigo está aquí.  
> Your friend is here.

The accent changes the meaning.

Don't try to memorize the whole song as random vocabulary.

Steal these **Spanish patterns**:

### Pattern 1

**noun + que + verb**

> el aire **que respiro**  
> the air that I breathe

### Pattern 2

**ir + a + infinitive**

> **vas a dar**  
> you're going to give

### Pattern 3

**verb + object pronoun**

> **mirarlo**  
> to look at it

### Pattern 4

**basta con + infinitive**

> **basta con mirar**  
> it's enough to look

### Pattern 5

**possessive adjective**

> **tu** piel  
> your skin

### Pattern 6

**possessive pronoun**

> es **tuyo**

> **será**
